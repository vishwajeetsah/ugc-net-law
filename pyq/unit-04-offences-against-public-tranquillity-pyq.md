# PYQ Practice Set — Unit IV: Offences against Public Tranquillity (BNS ss. 189–197)

10 previous-year-style multiple-choice questions on Chapter XI of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Answers with explanations at the end. (Study notes for UGC NET Law, Code 58 — legal information only, not legal advice.)

---

**Q1.** The minimum number of persons required to constitute an unlawful assembly under BNS s. 189 is:

(a) two
(b) three
(c) five
(d) ten

**Q2.** Under BNS s. 191(3), a person guilty of rioting while armed with a deadly weapon is punishable with imprisonment which may extend to:

(a) two years
(b) three years
(c) five years
(d) seven years

**Q3.** A joins an unlawful assembly after it has been lawfully commanded to disperse, knowing of the command. He is punishable under:

(a) BNS s. 189(2)
(b) BNS s. 189(3)
(c) BNS s. 189(4)
(d) BNS s. 189(5)

**Q4.** Match the BNS sections with their IPC equivalents:

| List I (BNS) | List II (IPC) |
|---|---|
| A. s. 190 | 1. s. 153 |
| B. s. 191 | 2. s. 149 |
| C. s. 192 | 3. s. 146 |
| D. s. 194 | 4. s. 159 |

Codes: (a) A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4 (b) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4 (c) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3 (d) A-3, B-4, C-2, D-1

**Q5.** In *Ram Manohar Lohia v. State of Bihar* (1966), the Supreme Court described "law and order", "public order" and "security of the State" as:

(a) three unrelated concepts, each with its own test
(b) three concentric circles — law and order the largest, public order within it, security of the State the smallest
(c) three concentric circles — security of the State the largest, law and order the smallest
(d) three synonyms for the same concept

**Q6.** Which of the following is **NOT** a common object of an unlawful assembly under BNS s. 189(1)?

(a) To overawe by criminal force the Central Government or a State Government
(b) To resist the execution of any law or legal process
(c) To commit mischief, criminal trespass or any other offence
(d) To hold a peaceful protest demanding the repeal of a law

**Q7.** A and B fight each other in a public market, disturbing the public peace. They are liable for:

(a) rioting under BNS s. 191
(b) affray under BNS s. 194
(c) unlawful assembly under BNS s. 189
(d) no offence, because only two persons are involved

**Q8.** Under BNS s. 195(1), a person who assaults a public servant endeavouring to disperse an unlawful assembly is punishable with imprisonment up to three years and a fine which:

(a) may extend to ₹1,000
(b) shall not be less than ₹25,000
(c) shall not be less than ₹10,000
(d) is left entirely to the court's discretion, with no minimum

**Q9.** In *Bilal Ahmed Kaloo v. State of A.P.* (1997), the Supreme Court held that an offence under IPC s. 153A (now BNS s. 196) requires:

(a) only the utterance of words that hurt the sentiments of a community
(b) the intention to promote enmity between different groups — mere offensive words are not enough
(c) actual violence following the words
(d) proof that the accused is a member of a particular community

**Q10.** Which clause of BNS s. 197(1) is **new** in the BNS, with no counterpart in IPC s. 153B?

(a) Imputation that a class of persons cannot bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India
(b) Assertion that a class of persons shall be denied their rights as citizens of India
(c) An appeal concerning the obligation of a class of persons, likely to cause disharmony
(d) Making or publishing false or misleading information, jeopardising the sovereignty, unity and integrity or security of India

---

## Answer Key

| Q | Answer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(c) five** | s. 189(1): "An assembly of five or more persons is designated an 'unlawful assembly'…" — four persons can never form one. |
| 2 | **(c) five years** | s. 191(3): rioting armed with a deadly weapon — up to 5 years. This is a BNS change: IPC s. 148 capped it at 3 years. |
| 3 | **(b) s. 189(3)** | s. 189(3) punishes joining or continuing in an unlawful assembly knowing it has been commanded to disperse — up to 2 years. s. 189(2) is ordinary membership (6 months); s. 189(4) is the armed member; s. 189(5) is the assembly merely *likely to disturb the peace*, not an unlawful assembly. |
| 4 | **(a) A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4** | s. 190 ↔ IPC s. 149 (common-object liability); s. 191 ↔ IPC s. 146 (rioting); s. 192 ↔ IPC s. 153 (provocation); s. 194 ↔ IPC s. 159 (affray). |
| 5 | **(b)** | The 1966 *Lohia* judgment: law and order is the largest circle, public order sits inside it, security of the State is the smallest at the centre. Only conduct disturbing the even tempo of community life affects public order. |
| 6 | **(d)** | A peaceful protest is not a common object — none of the five objects in s. 189(1)(a)–(e) covers peaceful dissent; they all require criminal force, resistance to law, or commission of an offence. |
| 7 | **(b) affray under BNS s. 194** | Affray needs only two or more persons fighting in a public place (up to 1 month / ₹1,000 fine). Rioting needs an unlawful assembly of five or more; two persons cannot riot. |
| 8 | **(b) shall not be less than ₹25,000** | s. 195(1): imprisonment up to 3 years, "or with fine which shall not be less than twenty-five thousand rupees, or with both" — the minimum fine is new in the BNS; IPC s. 152 had none. |
| 9 | **(b)** | *Bilal Ahmed Kaloo* (1997): s. 153A requires the intention to promote enmity; the prosecution must prove the mens rea, not merely that the words were offensive. |
| 10 | **(d)** | s. 197(1)(d) — false or misleading information jeopardising sovereignty, unity and integrity or security — is new in the BNS; IPC s. 153B had no equivalent clause. |

**Score guide:** 9–10 — exam-ready on this chapter; 7–8 — re-read §2 (s. 189 sub-sections) and §9–10 (ss. 196–197); below 7 — re-read the full note, especially the chapter-at-a-glance table and the *Lohia* section.
