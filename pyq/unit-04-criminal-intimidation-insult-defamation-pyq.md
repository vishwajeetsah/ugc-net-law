# PYQ Practice Set — Criminal Intimidation, Insult and Defamation (BNS ss. 351–357)

10 previous-year-style multiple-choice questions on Unit IV, Chapter XIX of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, with an answer key and explanations. Covers the IPC → BNS mapping, the punishment ladders, the ten Exceptions to defamation, and the leading cases.

---

**Q1.** Which of the following IPC sections did **not** move into Chapter XIX of the BNS, 2023?

(a) Section 503 — criminal intimidation
(b) Section 504 — intentional insult
(c) Section 509 — word, gesture or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman
(d) Section 510 — misconduct in public by a drunken person

**Q2.** Under BNS s. 351(3), criminal intimidation is punishable with imprisonment up to seven years where the threat is to cause death or grievous hurt, or to cause destruction of property by fire, or to cause an offence punishable with death or life imprisonment or up to seven years, or to —

(a) impute unchastity to a woman
(b) insult the modesty of a woman
(c) cause alarm to a public servant
(d) commit an offence against the State

**Q3.** A posts abusive comments about a police officer on Facebook, but the comments contain no threat of injury to the officer's person, reputation or property. Following *Manik Taneja v. State of Karnataka* (2015), the correct position is that —

(a) A is guilty of criminal intimidation under s. 351 BNS
(b) A is guilty of intentional insult under s. 352 BNS
(c) A is not guilty of criminal intimidation, because mere abuse without a threat of injury does not satisfy s. 351
(d) A is guilty of defamation under s. 356 BNS

**Q4.** Match the BNS section with its IPC counterpart:

| List I (BNS) | List II (IPC) |
|---|---|
| A. s. 353 | 1. s. 508 |
| B. s. 354 | 2. s. 505 |
| C. s. 355 | 3. s. 499 |
| D. s. 356 | 4. s. 510 |

(a) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
(b) A-2, B-4, C-1, D-3
(c) A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
(d) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2

**Q5.** Which of the following statements about BNS s. 353 is **correct**?

(a) Section 353(1)(b) requires the statement to be false
(b) Section 353(2) punishes statements creating enmity between groups only if they contain false information, rumour or alarming news
(c) An offence under s. 353(2) committed in a place of worship is punishable with imprisonment up to three years
(d) There is no exception to s. 353

**Q6.** "Truth alone is not a defence to criminal defamation." This statement refers to which Exception to s. 356(1) BNS?

(a) Exception 2 — opinion on a public servant's conduct
(b) Exception 1 — truth for the public good
(c) Exception 9 — imputation for the protection of interests
(d) Exception 4 — true report of court proceedings

**Q7.** In *Subramanian Swamy v. Union of India* (2016), the Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of criminal defamation (IPC ss. 499–500, now BNS s. 356) on the ground that —

(a) reputation is not a fundamental right and Article 19(2) does not mention criminal defamation
(b) reputation is a part of the right to life under Article 21, and "defamation" in Article 19(2) covers criminal as well as civil defamation
(c) criminal defamation is saved by Article 19(1)(a) itself
(d) the ten Exceptions to s. 499 IPC are unconstitutional and were read down

**Q8.** Which of the following is **not** one of the ten Exceptions to defamation under BNS s. 356(1)?

(a) Imputation which is true and made for the public good
(b) Good-faith opinion on the merits of a performance submitted to the judgment of the public
(c) Imputation made in the course of a parliamentary debate, protected by Article 105
(d) Good-faith censure by a person having lawful authority over another

**Q9.** A, in a state of intoxication, appears in a public place and behaves in a manner that annoys B. Under BNS s. 355, A may be punished with —

(a) rigorous imprisonment up to one year
(b) simple imprisonment up to twenty-four hours, or fine up to ₹1,000, or both, or community service
(c) imprisonment up to two years, or fine, or both
(d) fine only, up to ₹5,000

**Q10.** Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?

(a) s. 351(4) BNS — anonymous communication — additional imprisonment up to two years
(b) s. 354 BNS — act endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India
(c) s. 357 BNS — misconduct in public by a drunken person
(d) s. 356(3) BNS — defamation — community service available

---

## Answer Key

**Q1 — (c).** IPC s. 509 (word, gesture or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman) moved to **BNS s. 79** in Chapter V (offences against women and children), not into Chapter XIX. Sections 503, 504 and 510 became ss. 351, 352 and 355 respectively.

**Q2 — (a).** Section 351(3) lists four aggravated forms: threat to cause death or grievous hurt; threat to cause destruction of property by fire; threat to cause an offence punishable with death or life imprisonment or up to seven years; and **imputing unchastity to a woman** — all punishable with up to seven years.

**Q3 — (c).** In *Manik Taneja* (2015) the Supreme Court quashed proceedings under s. 506 IPC: abusive words, without a threat of injury to person, reputation or property, do not amount to criminal intimidation. Option (b) is wrong because s. 352 needs an insult intended or known to be likely to provoke a breach of the peace, which was not made out on those facts; option (d) is wrong because the comments were not shown to be an imputation published to a third person harming reputation.

**Q4 — (a).** s. 353 = IPC s. 505 (statements conducing to public mischief); s. 354 = IPC s. 508 (Divine displeasure); s. 355 = IPC s. 510 (drunken misconduct); s. 356 = IPC ss. 499–502 (defamation).

**Q5 — (b).** Section 353(2) requires the statement or report to contain **false information, rumour or alarming news** creating enmity, hatred or ill will between groups. (a) is wrong: s. 353(1)(b) needs fear or alarm inducing an offence against the State or public tranquillity — the statement need not be false. (c) is wrong: s. 353(3) raises the punishment to **five years** in a place of worship. (d) is wrong: s. 353 has an Exception — reasonable grounds for believing the statement true, made in good faith and without the prohibited intent.

**Q6 — (b).** Exception 1 protects an imputation which is **true, if it be for the public good** that it should be made or published. Truth without public good is not a defence — a true statement published out of spite, with no public good, is defamation.

**Q7 — (b).** A 2-judge bench (Misra and Nariman, JJ.) held that reputation is part of the right to life under **Article 21**, that "defamation" in **Article 19(2)** covers criminal as well as civil defamation, and that the ten Exceptions keep the offence within reasonable restriction.

**Q8 — (c).** Parliamentary privilege under Article 105 is a constitutional protection, not one of the ten Exceptions to s. 356(1). The ten Exceptions are: (1) truth for the public good; (2) public servant's conduct; (3) public question; (4) true court report; (5) merits of a decided case; (6) performance before the public; (7) censure by lawful authority; (8) accusation to lawful authority; (9) imputation for protection of interests; (10) caution in good faith.

**Q9 — (b).** Section 355: simple imprisonment up to **twenty-four hours**, or fine up to **₹1,000**, or both, **or community service** — one of only two sections in the chapter where the BNS adds community service (the other is s. 356(2)).

**Q10 — (a).** s. 351(4) punishes intimidation by anonymous communication with up to two years **in addition to** the punishment under sub-section (1). (b) is wrong: s. 354 is the Divine displeasure offence (IPC s. 508); (c) is wrong: s. 357 is breach of contract to attend a helpless person (IPC s. 491), while drunken misconduct is s. 355; (d) is wrong: community service is available under s. 356(2) for the defamer, but **not** under s. 356(3) for the printer or s. 356(4) for the seller.
