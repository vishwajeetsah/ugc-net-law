# PYQ Practice Set — Unit IV: Offences against the State (BNS ss. 147–158)

10 previous-year-style multiple-choice questions on Chapter VII of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Answers with explanations at the end. (Study notes for UGC NET Law, Code 58 — legal information only, not legal advice.)

---

**Q1.** Which section of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 replaces IPC s. 124A (sedition)?

(a) s. 147
(b) s. 150
(c) s. 152
(d) s. 153

**Q2.** Under BNS s. 152, the mental element required is that the accused acted:

(a) dishonestly
(b) purposely or knowingly
(c) negligently
(d) recklessly

**Q3.** A, a public servant in charge of a prisoner of war, allows the prisoner to escape through sheer carelessness, without any intention to help him. A is liable under:

(a) BNS s. 156
(b) BNS s. 157
(c) BNS s. 158
(d) BNS s. 150

**Q4.** Match the BNS sections with their IPC equivalents:

| List I (BNS) | List II (IPC) |
|---|---|
| A. s. 147 | 1. s. 121A |
| B. s. 148 | 2. s. 122 |

Codes: (a) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4 (b) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2 (c) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4 (d) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4

**Q5.** In *Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar* (1962), the Supreme Court:

(a) struck down s. 124A IPC as unconstitutional
(b) upheld s. 124A IPC but read it down to acts with the intention or tendency to create disorder or incitement to violence
(c) held that mere criticism of the Government is sedition
(d) held that s. 124A IPC applies only to the armed forces

**Q6.** Which of the following is **NOT** within BNS s. 152?

(a) Exciting secession from India
(b) Encouraging feelings of separatist activities
(c) Strong criticism of a government policy in a newspaper editorial
(d) Endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India by electronic communication

**Q7.** The maximum punishment under BNS s. 147 (waging war against the Government of India) is:

(a) imprisonment for life
(b) death
(c) imprisonment up to 10 years
(d) imprisonment up to 7 years

**Q8.** In *Balwant Singh v. State of Punjab* (1995), the Supreme Court held that raising slogans such as "Khalistan Zindabad" was:

(a) sedition, because the slogans were raised on the day of the Prime Minister's assassination
(b) not sedition, as the casual raising of slogans without incitement to violence does not satisfy the *Kedar Nath* test
(c) an offence under s. 153A IPC only
(d) punishable as waging war under s. 121 IPC

**Q9.** Previous sanction of the Central Government is required for prosecution of offences against the State under:

(a) BNSS s. 222
(b) BNSS s. 218
(c) BNS s. 152 itself
(d) Article 361 of the Constitution

**Q10.** A collects arms and ammunition intending to wage war against the Government of India, but is arrested before any attack. He is liable under:

(a) BNS s. 147, because preparation is treated as waging war
(b) BNS s. 149, which punishes preparation to wage war as a substantive offence
(c) BNS s. 62 (attempt) only
(d) No offence, because preparation is generally not punishable

---

## Answer Key

| Q | Answer | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(c) s. 152** | BNS s. 152 — "acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India" — replaces the deleted IPC s. 124A. |
| 2 | **(b) purposely or knowingly** | s. 152 expressly requires that the accused acted "purposely or knowingly" — a higher, express mental element than s. 124A carried. |
| 3 | **(b) BNS s. 157** | s. 157 punishes a public servant *negligently* suffering a prisoner of State/war to escape (simple imprisonment up to 3 years). s. 156 is the *voluntary* allowance (life or up to 10 years); s. 158 is aiding/rescuing/harbouring by any person. |
| 4 | **(a) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4** | s. 147 ↔ 121 (waging war); s. 148 ↔ 121A (conspiracy); s. 149 ↔ 122 (collecting arms/preparation); s. 150 ↔ 123 (concealment). |
| 5 | **(b)** | The 5-judge bench upheld s. 124A but confined it to acts with the intention or tendency to create disorder, disturbance of law and order, or incitement to violence. |
| 6 | **(c)** | Strong criticism of government policy does not excite secession, armed rebellion or separatism, nor endanger sovereignty/unity/integrity — it is outside s. 152. (Under s. 124A the old charge was "disaffection towards the Government"; s. 152 drops that target.) |
| 7 | **(b) death** | s. 147 is the only section in the chapter carrying death (death or life imprisonment + fine). |
| 8 | **(b)** | *Balwant Singh* (1995): casual sloganeering, without incitement, is not sedition under the *Kedar Nath* test. |
| 9 | **(a) BNSS s. 222** | Cognizance of offences against the State needs the previous sanction of the Central Government (BNSS s. 222; CrPC s. 196). |
| 10 | **(b) BNS s. 149** | Preparation to wage war is one of the few places where preparation is a substantive offence — s. 149 (IPC s. 122), life or up to 10 years + fine. |

**Score guide:** 9–10 — exam-ready on this chapter; 7–8 — re-read §4 (s. 152) and §5 (case law); below 7 — re-read the full note, especially the s. 147–158 table.
