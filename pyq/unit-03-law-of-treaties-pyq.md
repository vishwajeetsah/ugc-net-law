# PYQ Practice Set — Unit III: The Law of Treaties (VCLT 1969)

10 previous-year-style multiple-choice questions with an answer key. Based on the note `units/unit-03-public-international-law/law-of-treaties.md`. Covers the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, 1969 (Arts. 2, 7, 18–21, 26–27, 31–32, 34–36, 53, 60–62), the ICJ's treaty cases, and India's position on the Convention.

> Study note — legal information for exam preparation, not legal advice.

## Questions

**Q.1.** Under Article 2(1)(a) of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, 1969, a "treaty" is an international agreement concluded between States in written form and governed by international law. Which of the following is **NOT** an element of this definition?

(a) The agreement is concluded between States
(b) The agreement is in written form
(c) The agreement is governed by international law
(d) The agreement is registered with the United Nations Secretariat

**Q.2.** Which of the following persons may represent a State for the purpose of adopting or authenticating the text of a treaty, or expressing the consent of the State to be bound by a treaty, **without** having to produce full powers?

(a) The head of a diplomatic mission, for all purposes
(b) The Head of State, the Head of Government, and the Minister for Foreign Affairs
(c) The Permanent Representative to the United Nations, for all purposes
(d) A minister of any department of the Government

**Q.3.** A State has signed a treaty subject to ratification. Before ratification, it takes steps that would defeat the object and purpose of the treaty. Under the VCLT, which Article is violated?

(a) Article 26
(b) Article 18
(c) Article 27
(d) Article 46

**Q.4.** Consider the following statements about reservations under the VCLT:

1. A reservation incompatible with the object and purpose of the treaty is not permitted.
2. If a treaty expressly authorises a particular reservation, no further acceptance by other parties is required.
3. A reservation is deemed accepted if no objection is raised within twelve months of its notification.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q.5.** "Every treaty in force is binding upon the parties to it and must be performed by them in good faith." This principle, *pacta sunt servanda*, is laid down in which Article of the VCLT?

(a) Article 27
(b) Article 26
(c) Article 31
(d) Article 34

**Q.6.** A State fails to perform a treaty obligation and pleads that its domestic legislation prevents performance. Which of the following is the correct position under the VCLT?

(a) The plea succeeds, because municipal law prevails over international law
(b) The plea succeeds only if the domestic legislation was enacted after the treaty
(c) The plea fails: a party may not invoke its internal law as justification for failure to perform a treaty
(d) The plea fails only if the treaty is a law-making treaty

**Q.7.** Consider the following statements about the interpretation of treaties under the VCLT:

1. Article 31 requires interpretation in good faith, in accordance with the ordinary meaning of the terms, in their context, and in the light of the treaty's object and purpose.
2. The preparatory work of the treaty (*travaux préparatoires*) is the primary means of interpretation under Article 32.
3. If a treaty is authenticated in two or more languages, each text is equally authoritative.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 3 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q.8.** Under Article 62 of the VCLT, a fundamental change of circumstances (*rebus sic stantibus*) may be invoked to terminate or withdraw from a treaty only if certain conditions are met. Which of the following is **NOT** a condition or limitation under Article 62?

(a) The circumstances constituted an essential basis of the consent of the parties to be bound
(b) The change radically transforms the extent of the obligations still to be performed
(c) The treaty must not be one establishing a boundary
(d) The change must have been caused by the act of a third State

**Q.9.** In the *Gabcikovo-Nagymaros Project* case (Hungary v. Slovakia, ICJ, 1997), the Court held that:

(a) Hungary was entitled to terminate the 1977 Treaty because of a fundamental change of circumstances
(b) The 1977 Treaty remained in force; neither supervening impossibility nor fundamental change of circumstances was established
(c) Slovakia's unilateral diversion of the Danube ("Variant C") was lawful
(d) The treaty was void because it conflicted with a peremptory norm of general international law

**Q.10.** Which of the following statements about India and the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, 1969 is **correct**?

(a) India has signed but not ratified the Convention
(b) India is neither a signatory nor a party to the Convention, but follows its provisions in practice as customary international law
(c) India ratified the Convention in 1980, the year it entered into force
(d) India is a party to the Convention but has entered a reservation to Article 31

---

## Answer Key

**Q.1 — (d).** Registration with the UN Secretariat (Art. 102, UN Charter; Art. 80, VCLT) is a separate requirement and is **not** part of the Art. 2(1)(a) definition. The definition requires: between States, in written form, governed by international law — whatever its designation. An unregistered treaty is still a treaty; it simply cannot be invoked before a UN organ.

**Q.2 — (b).** Article 7(2)(a): Heads of State, Heads of Government and Ministers for Foreign Affairs are deemed to represent their State without full powers. Heads of diplomatic mission (option (a)) may only **adopt** the text of a treaty between the accrediting and receiving State, and Permanent Representatives only the text of a treaty adopted within the conference or organisation — neither may express consent to be bound without full powers.

**Q.3 — (b).** Article 18 imposes the interim obligation to refrain from acts which would defeat the object and purpose of a treaty after signature (subject to ratification) or after consent pending entry into force. Article 26 is pacta sunt servanda (performance of treaties **in force**); Article 27 is the bar on invoking internal law; Article 46 concerns invalidity for violation of internal law on competence.

**Q.4 — (d).** All three statements are correct. Statement 1: Art. 19(c) — a reservation incompatible with the object and purpose is impermissible (the test from the ICJ's *Reservations to the Genocide Convention* advisory opinion, 1951). Statement 2: Art. 20(1) — an expressly authorised reservation needs no further acceptance. Statement 3: Art. 20(5) — the twelve-month rule: silence for 12 months after notification (or after expression of consent, whichever is later) amounts to acceptance.

**Q.5 — (b).** Article 26. Article 27 is the prohibition on invoking internal law; Article 31 is the general rule of interpretation; Article 34 is the rule that a treaty creates no obligations or rights for a third State without its consent.

**Q.6 — (c).** Article 27 VCLT: "A party may not invoke the provisions of its internal law as justification for its failure to perform a treaty." The only qualification is Article 46 (manifest violation of a rule of internal law of fundamental importance concerning competence to conclude treaties, invoked to invalidate consent — not to excuse non-performance).

**Q.7 — (a).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct; statement 2 is wrong. Article 32 makes the preparatory work a **supplementary** means of interpretation — used to confirm the Art. 31 meaning, or where Art. 31 leaves the meaning ambiguous or leads to a manifestly absurd result. It is never the primary means. Statement 3 reflects Art. 33(1): texts authenticated in two or more languages are equally authoritative.

**Q.8 — (d).** Article 62(1) requires (a) the circumstances constituted an essential basis of consent and (b) the change radically transforms the obligations still to be performed; Article 62(2)(a) excludes treaties establishing a boundary, and 62(2)(b) excludes cases where the change results from a breach by the invoking party. There is no requirement that the change be caused by a third State — the change can be political, economic or environmental, as in *Gabcikovo-Nagymaros* (1997), where the Court still rejected the plea because the two positive conditions were not met.

**Q.9 — (b).** The ICJ held the 1977 Treaty remained in force: Hungary had not established supervening impossibility (Art. 61) or a fundamental change of circumstances (Art. 62), and its own abandonment of the works could not be relied on. Slovakia's Variant C was a breach of the treaty (so option (c) is wrong), but that breach did not entitle Hungary to terminate it. The parties were required to negotiate in good faith to achieve the treaty's objectives.

**Q.10 — (b).** India is neither a signatory nor a party to the VCLT; it follows the Convention's provisions in practice as customary international law (MEA, Legal & Treaties Division, SoP on the conclusion of treaties). Indian courts apply its rules as custom — e.g. the Delhi High Court in *AWAS Ireland v. Directorate General of Civil Aviation* (2015) relied on Articles 26, 27 and 31. Options (a), (c) and (d) are all factually wrong.
