# Unit IV — PYQ Practice Set: Offences against the Human Body (BNS ss. 100–106)

10 previous-year-style questions with an answer key. Topic: culpable homicide and murder under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Based on the note `units/unit-04-law-of-crimes/offences-against-human-body.md`.

## Questions

**1.** Under the BNS, which of the following is NOT one of the four clauses of murder under s. 101?
(a) Intention of causing death
(b) Intention of causing bodily injury known to be likely to cause death
(c) Knowledge that the act is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death
(d) Intention of causing such bodily injury as is likely to cause death, without knowing that it is sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death

**2.** A shoots at B with intent to kill him, misses, and the bullet kills C, a bystander. Under the BNS, A's liability for C's death is governed by:
(a) s. 100 — culpable homicide only
(b) s. 102 — transferred malice, of the same description as if he had killed B
(c) s. 106 — causing death by negligence
(d) s. 104 — mob lynching

**3.** In *Virsa Singh v. State of Punjab* (1958), the Supreme Court held that under clause thirdly of s. 300 IPC (now s. 101 BNS), the question whether the bodily injury inflicted was sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death is:
(a) A subjective question, judged by what the accused believed about the injury
(b) An objective question, judged by the nature of the injury itself
(c) A question for the jury alone, not the court
(d) Irrelevant once intention to cause the injury is proved

**4.** Which exception to murder under s. 101 BNS was at issue in *K.M. Nanavati v. State of Maharashtra* (1962)?
(a) Private defence
(b) Sudden fight
(c) Grave and sudden provocation
(d) Death with consent

**5.** A group of six persons acting in concert lynches a man because of his caste. Under the BNS, this is punishable under:
(a) s. 103 only — ordinary murder
(b) s. 104 — murder by a group of five or more persons on the ground of caste, punishable with death or life imprisonment
(c) s. 105 — culpable homicide not amounting to murder
(d) s. 111 — organised crime

**6.** Under BNS s. 106(1), the maximum punishment for causing death by a rash or negligent act not amounting to culpable homicide is:
(a) 2 years — the same as IPC s. 304A
(b) 3 years
(c) 5 years
(d) 7 years

**7.** A public servant, acting in good faith for the advancement of public justice, exceeds the powers given to him by law and causes a death, without ill-will towards the deceased. This falls under which exception to murder?
(a) Exception 1 — grave and sudden provocation
(b) Exception 2 — private defence
(c) Exception 3 — public servant exceeding powers
(d) Exception 4 — sudden fight

**8.** In *Jacob Mathew v. State of Punjab* (2005), the Supreme Court held that a doctor is criminally liable for negligence only when the negligence is:
(a) Simple lack of care
(b) An error of judgment
(c) Gross negligence
(d) Proved by the patient's complaint alone

**9.** Where one of the five exceptions to s. 101 BNS applies, the offence becomes:
(a) No offence at all — the accused is acquitted
(b) Culpable homicide not amounting to murder, punishable under s. 105
(c) Causing death by negligence under s. 106
(d) Attempt to commit culpable homicide under s. 110

**10.** Match the BNS provision with its IPC equivalent:
(i) s. 100 (A) s. 302
(ii) s. 101 (B) s. 299
(iii) s. 103 (C) s. 304
(iv) s. 105 (D) s. 300
(a) (i)-(B), (ii)-(D), (iii)-(A), (iv)-(C)
(b) (i)-(D), (ii)-(B), (iii)-(C), (iv)-(A)
(c) (i)-(B), (ii)-(A), (iii)-(D), (iv)-(C)
(d) (i)-(A), (ii)-(D), (iii)-(B), (iv)-(C)

## Answer Key

**1. (d)** — Clauses: (1) intention to cause death; (2) intention to cause injury *known* to be likely to cause death; (3) intention to cause injury *sufficient in the ordinary course of nature* to cause death; (4) knowledge of imminent danger. Option (d) describes no clause — it mixes clause (b) of s. 100 (culpable homicide) with clause thirdly.

**2. (b)** — s. 102 (IPC s. 301), transferred malice: the culpable homicide is of the same description as if A had killed B. *R v. Latimer* (1886) is the English authority.

**3. (b)** — *Virsa Singh* (1958): sufficiency is judged objectively by the injury itself. The accused need only have intended the injury; he need not have known it was sufficient to cause death.

**4. (c)** — Exception 1, grave and sudden provocation. The Court held it did not apply because the interval between the provocation and the shooting was a cooling period in which a reasonable man would have regained self-control.

**5. (b)** — s. 104: a group of five or more persons acting in concert committing murder on the ground of race, caste or community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief — death or life imprisonment + fine. The provision follows *Tehseen Poonawalla v. Union of India* (2018).

**6. (c)** — 5 years + fine. The BNS raised the maximum from 2 years under IPC s. 304A to 5 years under s. 106(1).

**7. (c)** — Exception 3 to s. 101: a public servant exceeding powers in good faith, without ill-will, for the advancement of public justice.

**8. (c)** — Gross negligence only. A simple lack of care or an error of judgment is not a crime; a private complaint should not be entertained without a credible expert opinion.

**9. (b)** — The five exceptions reduce murder to culpable homicide not amounting to murder (s. 105). They never make the killing no offence at all — that is the function of the general exceptions in Chapter III (ss. 14–44).

**10. (a)** — s. 100 = IPC s. 299 (culpable homicide); s. 101 = IPC s. 300 (murder); s. 103 = IPC s. 302 (punishment for murder); s. 105 = IPC s. 304 (punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder).
