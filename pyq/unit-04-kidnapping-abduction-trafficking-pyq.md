# PYQ Practice Set — Kidnapping, Abduction and Trafficking (BNS ss. 137–146)

10 previous-year-style MCQs on Unit IV (Law of Crimes), with an answer key and explanations. All section numbers are from the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (in force 1 July 2024); IPC equivalents are given in the explanations.

---

**Q1.** Under the BNS, kidnapping is of how many kinds, and which are they?

(a) Three kinds — from India, from lawful guardianship, and from a foreign country
(b) Two kinds — from India and from lawful guardianship
(c) One kind — from lawful guardianship only
(d) Four kinds — from India, from guardianship, by force, and by deceit

**Q2.** A 17-year-old girl leaves her father's house of her own accord and joins a young man, who simply allows her to accompany him. On the ratio of *S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras* (1965), the young man is:

(a) Guilty of kidnapping from lawful guardianship, because her consent is irrelevant
(b) Not guilty of kidnapping, because there was no "taking" or "enticing" by him
(c) Guilty of abduction, because he induced her by deceitful means
(d) Guilty of kidnapping from India, because she crossed a State boundary

**Q3.** Which of the following is **correct** about abduction under s. 138 BNS?

(a) Abduction is punishable in itself with imprisonment up to 7 years
(b) Abduction can only be of a child below 16 years
(c) Abduction by itself is not punishable; it is punished only when done for a purpose the law names, such as ransom or murder
(d) Abduction requires the victim to be taken out of the keeping of a lawful guardian

**Q4.** Kidnapping or abducting a person for ransom, under s. 140(2) BNS, is punishable with:

(a) Imprisonment up to 7 years and fine
(b) Imprisonment for life, or rigorous imprisonment up to 10 years, and fine
(c) Death, or imprisonment for life, and fine
(d) Rigorous imprisonment of 10 years to life, and fine

**Q5.** Under s. 143 BNS (trafficking of a person), the punishment for trafficking **more than one minor** is:

(a) Rigorous imprisonment of 7–10 years and fine
(b) Rigorous imprisonment of 10 years to life and fine
(c) Rigorous imprisonment of 14 years to life and fine
(d) Imprisonment for life (remainder of natural life) and fine

**Q6.** Which pair is **correctly** matched?

(a) s. 139 BNS — trafficking of a person
(b) s. 141 BNS — importation of a girl under 21 years or a boy under 18 years from a foreign country
(c) s. 144 BNS — habitual dealing in slaves
(d) s. 146 BNS — exploitation of a trafficked person

**Q7.** In *People's Union for Democratic Rights v. Union of India* (1982), the Supreme Court held that "forced labour" under Article 23 of the Constitution includes:

(a) Only labour extracted by physical force or threat of physical force
(b) Labour exacted for less than the minimum wage, including labour extracted by economic compulsion
(c) Only bonded labour arising from a debt
(d) Only labour of children below 14 years

**Q8.** A person knowingly conceals a kidnapped child in his house. Under s. 142 BNS, his punishment is:

(a) The same punishment as the kidnapping or abduction itself
(b) Imprisonment up to 1 year, or fine, or both
(c) Rigorous imprisonment of 5–7 years and fine
(d) No punishment, because concealment after the taking is not an offence

**Q9.** Which statement about s. 140 BNS is **incorrect**?

(a) s. 140(1) covers kidnapping or abducting in order to murder — life or up to 10 years
(b) s. 140(3) covers kidnapping or abducting to be secretly and wrongfully confined — up to 7 years
(c) s. 140(4) covers kidnapping or abducting to subject the person to grievous hurt, slavery or unnatural lust — up to 10 years
(d) s. 140(2) covers kidnapping or abducting for ransom — rigorous imprisonment of 7–10 years

**Q10.** *Thakorlal D. Vadgama v. State of Gujarat* (1973) is authority for the proposition that:

(a) A minor's consent is a complete defence to kidnapping from lawful guardianship
(b) Persuading or inducing a minor to leave the guardian's keeping amounts to "taking" under the kidnapping provisions
(c) Merely allowing a minor to accompany the accused is "taking"
(d) Kidnapping from India requires the victim to be taken beyond the limits of India by force

---

## Answer Key

| Q | Answer | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(b)** | s. 137(1): two kinds — (a) from India, (b) from lawful guardianship |
| 2 | **(b)** | *Varadarajan* (1965): no "taking" where the minor leaves on her own; the accused's active role is essential |
| 3 | **(c)** | s. 138 defines abduction; it is punishable only through a purpose-section (ss. 87, 97, 140) |
| 4 | **(c)** | s. 140(2): death, or imprisonment for life, and fine |
| 5 | **(c)** | s. 143(4): trafficking of more than one minor — RI 14 years to life + fine |
| 6 | **(b)** | s. 141 = importation (IPC s. 366B); s. 139 = child for begging; s. 144 = exploitation of trafficked person; s. 145 = habitual slave dealing; s. 146 = unlawful compulsory labour |
| 7 | **(b)** | *PUDR* (1982): forced labour under Art. 23 includes labour for less than the minimum wage / economic compulsion |
| 8 | **(a)** | s. 142: the concealer is punished with the same punishment as the kidnapping or abduction |
| 9 | **(d)** | s. 140(2) (ransom) is death or life, not 7–10 years; 7–10 years is the s. 143(1) trafficking range |
| 10 | **(b)** | *Vadgama* (1973): inducement/persuasion of a minor to leave is "taking"; contrast *Varadarajan* and *Raja Ram* (1973) |

## Explanations

- **Q1:** s. 137(1) BNS (IPC ss. 359–361) recognises exactly two kinds of kidnapping. Importation from a foreign country is a separate offence (s. 141), not a third kind of kidnapping.
- **Q2:** *S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras*, AIR 1965 SC 942 — the girl left on her own; the accused did no act of taking or enticing. The Supreme Court acquitted. Her consent was irrelevant in principle, but on these facts there was no "taking" at all.
- **Q3:** Abduction (s. 138, IPC s. 362) is a definition, not a punishable offence by itself. It becomes punishable only when the purpose brings it under s. 87, s. 97 or s. 140.
- **Q4:** s. 140(2) (IPC s. 364A, inserted 1993) carries the heaviest punishment in the cluster — death or life — because ransom kidnapping endangers life and public order together.
- **Q5:** The s. 143 ladder: (1) 7–10 years; (2) more than one person, 10 years–life; (3) a minor, 10 years–life; (4) more than one minor, 14 years–life; (5) repeat offender, life.
- **Q6:** The cluster maps as: s. 139 begging (IPC 363A); s. 140 aggravated forms (IPC 364/364A/365/367); s. 141 importation (IPC 366B); s. 142 concealment (IPC 368); s. 143 trafficking (IPC 370); s. 144 exploitation (IPC 370A); s. 145 slave dealing (IPC 371); s. 146 compulsory labour (IPC 374).
- **Q7:** *PUDR v. Union of India* (1982) 3 SCC 235 — "forced labour" in Art. 23 is not confined to physical compulsion; work for less than the minimum wage, extracted because the worker has no real choice, is forced labour (begar).
- **Q8:** s. 142 (IPC s. 368) expressly provides that the concealer "shall be punished with the punishment provided for kidnapping or abduction" — the punishment follows the underlying offence.
- **Q9:** Option (d) is wrong: s. 140(2) is death or life. The 7–10 year figure belongs to s. 143(1) (trafficking of a person).
- **Q10:** *Thakorlal D. Vadgama v. State of Gujarat*, AIR 1973 SC 2313 — "taking" includes inducing a minor by persuasion to leave the guardian's keeping. Option (c) states the *Varadarajan*/*Raja Ram* position, which is the opposite rule.
