# PYQ Practice Set — Unit III: Nature, Sources and Subjects of International Law

10 previous-year-style questions on the nature, sources and subjects of public international law, with an answer key. Questions are modelled on the format of UGC NET Law Paper 2 (matching sets, assertion–reason, multi-statement) — the answers and explanations are original study material, not copied from any paper.

> Study note — legal information for exam preparation, not legal advice.

## Questions

**Q1.** Which of the following is **NOT** a source of international law listed in Article 38(1) of the Statute of the International Court of Justice?

(a) International conventions
(b) International custom
(c) Resolutions of the UN General Assembly
(d) General principles of law recognised by civilised nations

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about international custom:

(i) It requires both general state practice and *opinio juris*.
(ii) A persistent objector is bound by a customary rule once it has crystallised.
(iii) *Opinio juris* means the belief that the practice is legally required.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) (i) and (iii) only
(b) (i) only
(c) (i), (ii) and (iii)
(d) (ii) and (iii) only

**Q3.** In which case did the International Court of Justice lay down that a customary rule requires both widespread state practice and *opinio juris*?

(a) *S.S. Lotus* (1927)
(b) *North Sea Continental Shelf cases* (1969)
(c) *Asylum case* (1950)
(d) *Barcelona Traction* (1970)

**Q4.** Match List I (concept) with List II (case / instrument):

| List I | List II |
|---|---|
| A. Freedom of States unless prohibited | 1. *Reparation for Injuries* (1949) |
| B. International personality of the UN | 2. *Barcelona Traction* (1970) |
| C. Obligations *erga omnes* | 3. *S.S. Lotus* (1927) |
| D. *Jus cogens* | 4. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, Art. 53 |

(a) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
(b) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2
(c) A-3, B-2, C-1, D-4
(d) A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3

**Q5.** Assertion (A): A treaty which conflicts with a peremptory norm of general international law is void.

Reason (R): Article 53 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties, 1969 provides that a treaty is void if, at the time of its conclusion, it conflicts with a peremptory norm (*jus cogens*).

(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
(b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
(c) A is true but R is false
(d) A is false but R is true

**Q6.** Which of the following is **NOT** one of the four criteria of statehood under Article 1 of the Montevideo Convention, 1933?

(a) A permanent population
(b) A defined territory
(c) Recognition by the United Nations
(d) Capacity to enter into relations with other States

**Q7.** The Estrada Doctrine, a recurring theme in UGC NET Law papers (June 2023, June 2025), concerns:

(a) Withholding recognition from governments that come to power unconstitutionally
(b) Automatic recognition of a government in effective control, without passing judgment on its legitimacy
(c) Recognition of a State only after it is admitted to the UN
(d) The constitutive theory of statehood

**Q8.** Under the Constitution of India, the provision that directs the State to "foster respect for international law and treaty obligations" is:

(a) Article 51(c)
(b) Article 253
(c) Article 73
(d) Article 246

**Q9.** In *Gramophone Company of India v. Birendra Bahadur Pandey* (1984), the Supreme Court of India held that:

(a) International law automatically overrides any inconsistent Act of Parliament
(b) International law is part of municipal law unless it conflicts with an Act of Parliament, and statutes should be construed to avoid conflict with it
(c) Treaties require no legislation to be enforced in Indian courts
(d) Article 51(c) is enforceable in a court of law

**Q10.** Consider the following statements:

(i) Judicial decisions of the ICJ are a subsidiary means for the determination of rules of law under Article 38(1)(d) of the ICJ Statute.
(ii) A decision of the ICJ has binding force only between the parties and in respect of the particular case.
(iii) The teachings of the most highly qualified publicists are a primary source of international law.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) (i) and (ii) only
(b) (ii) and (iii) only
(c) (i), (ii) and (iii)
(d) (iii) only

---

## Answer key

| Q | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | (c) | Art. 38(1) lists conventions, custom, general principles and (as subsidiary means) judicial decisions and teachings. UNGA resolutions are not listed; they are evidence of *opinio juris* and can help form custom, but they are not a source in themselves. |
| 2 | (a) | (i) correct — the two-element test. (iii) correct — *opinio juris* is the belief of legal obligation. (ii) is false — a persistent objector that objected consistently while the rule was forming is **not** bound. |
| 3 | (b) | *North Sea Continental Shelf* (1969) is the locus classicus of the two-element test. *Lotus* (1927) is the freedom-unless-prohibited principle; *Asylum* (1950) is local custom; *Barcelona Traction* (1970) is *erga omnes*. |
| 4 | (a) | Lotus = freedom unless prohibited (A-3); *Reparation for Injuries* = UN's personality (B-1); *Barcelona Traction* = *erga omnes* (C-2); VCLT Art. 53 = *jus cogens* (D-4). |
| 5 | (a) | Both true; the Reason states the very provision (VCLT Art. 53) that makes the Assertion true. |
| 6 | (c) | The Montevideo criteria are permanent population, defined territory, government, and capacity to enter into relations. UN admission is not a criterion — recognition and statehood are separate questions. |
| 7 | (b) | Estrada Doctrine (Mexico, 1930): recognition is automatic; the recognising State does not judge the legitimacy of a new government, only whether it is in effective control. Option (a) describes the Tobar Doctrine. |
| 8 | (a) | Article 51(c) (DPSP) — "foster respect for international law and treaty obligations". Art. 253 gives Parliament the power to legislate to implement treaties. |
| 9 | (b) | *Gramophone Co.* (1984): international law forms part of municipal law except where it conflicts with an Act of Parliament; courts construe statutes to harmonise with international law where possible. |
| 10 | (a) | (i) correct — Art. 38(1)(d) is expressly a "subsidiary means". (ii) correct — Art. 59, ICJ Statute. (iii) is false — teachings are also only a subsidiary means, not a primary source. |
