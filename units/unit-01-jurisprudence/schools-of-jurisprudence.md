# Unit I — Jurisprudence: Schools of Jurisprudence

Revision note. The schools, their core claim, and their leading thinkers are asked in almost every cycle — most often as matching and assertion–reason questions. Hart and Austin have appeared in 2021, 2022 and 2023.

## 1. Analytical (Positivist) School

**Core claim:** Law is what is *posited* — a command of the sovereign, or a system of rules identified by a social test. Law as it **is**, not as it ought to be.

| Thinker | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Jeremy Bentham | Founder of legal positivism; "greatest happiness" utilitarianism; law as command of the sovereign |
| John Austin | *The Province of Jurisprudence Determined* (1832); law = command of the sovereign, backed by sanction; "every law is a command" |
| Hans Kelsen | *Pure Theory of Law*; law as a hierarchy of norms topped by the **Grundnorm**; separates law from morals, religion, politics |
| H.L.A. Hart | *The Concept of Law* (1961); **primary rules** (duty-imposing) + **secondary rules** (recognition, change, adjudication); the **rule of recognition** replaces Austin's sovereign |
| Joseph Raz | Service conception of authority; law claims legitimate authority |

**PYQ hook:** Hart's primary/secondary rules were asked in the Oct 2022 paper; Hart closes the Nov 2021 paper (comprehension passage on whether international law is binding law).

## 2. Historical School

**Core claim:** Law is *found*, not made — it grows from the customs and spirit of a people (**Volksgeist**).

| Thinker | Contribution |
|---|---|
| F.C. von Savigny | Founder; law grows with a people's consciousness like language; opposed codification in Germany |
| Georg Puchta | Concepts and institutions develop through juristic science |
| Sir Henry Maine | "From status to contract"; historical/comparative method in *Ancient Law* (1861) |

## 3. Natural Law School

**Core claim:** There exists a higher law — reason, morality, divine will — against which positive law is measured; an unjust law can fail as law.

| Phase | Thinkers / notes |
|---|---|
| Greek–Roman | Plato, Aristotle; Cicero — *jus gentium*, universal reason |
| Medieval | St. Thomas Aquinas — eternal, divine, natural and human law |
| Social contract | Grotius, Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau |
| Revival (20th c.) | Post-Nuremberg revival; Lon Fuller — "inner morality of law" (8 desiderata); John Finnis |

**PYQ hook:** A natural-law / *jus gentium* passage closed the Dec 2025 paper.

## 4. Sociological School

**Core claim:** Law is a social institution; study law in action, by its effects — "law in books" vs "law in action".

| Thinker | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Rudolf von Ihering | Law as the result of human purpose and struggle; "law is the politics of force" |
| Eugen Ehrlich | "Living law" of society; founder of sociology of law |
| Roscoe Pound | **Social engineering** — balancing of interests; classification of interests (individual, public, social) |
| Léon Duguit | Social solidarity; no rights, only social functions |

**PYQ hook:** Ihering was asked in the Dec 2025 paper.

## 5. Realist School

**Core claim:** Law is what courts *do*; rules on paper guarantee nothing. Certainty of law is a myth.

| Branch | Thinkers / notes |
|---|---|
| American | Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. — "the life of the law has not been logic: it has been experience"; Karl Llewellyn; Jerome Frank — fact-scepticism |
| Scandinavian | Alf Ross; Karl Olivecrona; Axel Hägerström |
