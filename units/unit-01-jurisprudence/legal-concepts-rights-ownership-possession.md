# Unit I — Jurisprudence: Legal Concepts — Rights, Duties, Ownership & Possession

Revision note. Unit I is the most predictable unit in the paper — the same core concepts rotate every cycle, most often as matching, assertion–reason and "which pair is NOT correct" questions. Rights, ownership and possession are asked in almost every cycle.

## 1. Rights

**Salmond:** a right is an interest recognised and protected by law. **Ihering:** a legal right is a **legally protected interest** — the interest is the substance, legal protection the form.

**Hohfeld's analysis (the PYQ favourite):** every legal relation is one of four pairs of correlatives and opposites.

| Correlative pair | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right — Duty | A has a right against B ⟺ B owes A a duty |
| Liberty (Privilege) — No-right | A is free to act ⟺ B has no right that A not act |
| Power — Liability | A can change B's legal position ⟺ B is liable to that change |
| Immunity — Disability | A is immune from B's power ⟺ B has no power over A |

**PYQ hook:** Hohfeld's correlatives were asked in Nov 2021 (Q.2) — "which pair is NOT correct?" Right–Duty, Liberty–No-right, Power–Liability, Immunity–Disability are all correct pairs; "Power–Liberty" is the wrong pair.

**Kinds of rights**

| Distinction | Types |
|---|---|
| Perfect / imperfect | Perfect — enforceable (duty is correlative and enforceable); imperfect — recognised but not enforceable (e.g. a time-barred debt) |
| In rem / in personam | In rem — against the world at large (ownership); in personam — against a specific person (a contractual claim) |
| Positive / negative | Positive — duty to do something; negative — duty to refrain |
| Principal / accessory | Principal — exists for its own sake; accessory — exists to secure another right |
| Vested / contingent | Vested — already accrued; contingent — depends on a future event |
| Proprietary / personal | Proprietary — over property, transferable, has economic value; personal — over status, not transferable |

## 2. Duties

A duty is the correlative of a right — an obligation to do or refrain from doing something, imposed by law. Austin classified duties as **absolute** (owed to the world at large, e.g. not to harm others) and **relative** (owed to a specific person, correlative to a right). A legal duty is distinct from a moral duty: only the former is enforceable by the State.

## 3. Ownership

**Salmond:** ownership is the relation between a person and a thing — the fullest right a person can have over a thing: a right **in rem**, **indefinite in point of user**, **unrestricted in point of disposition**, and **unlimited in point of duration**.

**Incidents of ownership** (the owner's bundle): right to possess, right to use and enjoy, right to alienate/dispose, right to exclude others, right to destroy (within legal limits).

**Kinds of ownership**

| Kind | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corporeal / incorporeal | Over a material thing (a house) / over an intangible (a copyright, a debt) |
| Sole / co-ownership | One owner / several owners of the same thing |
| Vested / contingent | Already complete / depends on a future event |
| Legal / equitable (trust) | Legal title with the trustee, beneficial interest with the beneficiary |
| Absolute / limited | Full ownership / ownership cut down by a charge, lease or other interest |

**PYQ hook:** ownership recurs across cycles (2009–2022 year-map), including an assertion–reason question on ownership as the right to exclude others.

## 4. Possession

**Salmond:** possession is the continuing exercise of a claim to the exclusive use of a thing. Its two elements are **corpus** (physical control) and **animus** (intention to exercise control).

| Theory | Claim |
|---|---|
| Savigny | Possession = corpus + animus; protection rests on the possessor's will |
| Ihering | Possession is generally the outward sign of ownership; animus matters only where the law requires a specific intent |
| Salmond | Possession is a fact and a right — protected because it is the evidence of ownership and because peace requires it |

**Kinds of possession:** corporeal (over a thing) / incorporeal (over a right); mediate (through another, e.g. a tenant) / immediate (direct); adverse possession — possession hostile to the true owner for the statutory period, which can ripen into title under the Limitation Act, 1963.

**PYQ hook:** possession as corpus + animus was asked in Oct 2022; finder cases and possession problems recur (2011 year-map).

## 5. Legal personality

A **person** in law is any being the law recognises as capable of rights and duties — natural persons and **juristic persons** (corporations, idols, institutions). Salmond: personality is the capacity to bear rights and duties. A **corporation sole** (e.g. a bishop, a public office) is a single person holding an office in perpetual succession.

**Case to remember:** *Shiromani Gurdwara Parbandhak Committee v Som Nath Dass* (2000) — the Supreme Court held that Sri Guru Granth Sahib is a juristic person capable of holding property.
