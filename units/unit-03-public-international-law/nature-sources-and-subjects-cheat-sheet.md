# Cheat Sheet — Public International Law: Nature, Sources and Subjects

One-page revision sheet for Unit III note 1. Companion note: `nature-sources-and-subjects.md`.

## Article 38(1), ICJ Statute — the sources (NOT a hierarchy)

| Item | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Conventions | Treaties — written agreements between States | Primary |
| (b) Custom | General practice accepted as law | Primary |
| (c) General principles | Principles common to domestic systems (good faith, estoppel, res judicata) | Primary, gap-filling |
| (d) Judicial decisions + teachings | ICJ decisions, publicists' writings | **Subsidiary means only** |

**Exception to the no-hierarchy rule:** *jus cogens* (VCLT Art. 53) prevails over any conflicting rule.

## Custom — the two-element test (*North Sea Continental Shelf*, 1969)

- **State practice (*usus*)** — widespread, representative, including specially-affected States
- **Opinio juris** — practice done from a sense of legal obligation
- **Persistent objector** — consistent objector during formation is NOT bound
- **Local custom** — *Asylum* (1950): must be proved by the State invoking it

## Statehood — Montevideo criteria (Art. 1, 1933)

**Permanent population · Defined territory · Government · Capacity to enter into relations**

## Recognition

| Doctrine / theory | One line |
|---|---|
| Declaratory theory | Recognition declares a fact; State exists once Montevideo criteria are met |
| Constitutive theory | Recognition creates the State |
| **Estrada Doctrine** (1930) | Automatic recognition of a government in effective control; no judgment on legitimacy — **PYQ: Jun 2023, Jun 2025** |
| Tobar Doctrine | Withhold recognition from unconstitutionally-installed governments |
| De jure vs de facto | Full/formal vs provisional recognition |

## Is international law "law"?

| Thinker | Position |
|---|---|
| Austin | Not law — "positive morality", no sovereign/sanction |
| Hart | Law, but a primitive system — lacks secondary rules (adjudication, change, sanction) |
| Kelsen | Law — sanction exists, decentralised |
| Modern view | Law — States treat it as binding; enforcement is decentralised, not absent |

## India — dualist in practice

| Provision / case | Point |
|---|---|
| Art. 51(c) | DPSP — foster respect for international law and treaty obligations |
| Art. 253 | Parliament legislates to implement treaties |
| *In re Berubari Union* (1960) | Treaty implementation needs legislation under Art. 253 |
| *Gramophone Co.* (1984) | International law is part of municipal law unless it conflicts with an Act of Parliament |
| *Vishaka* (1997) | International norms (CEDAW) fill a domestic vacuum — read into Arts. 14, 15, 21 |
| *Jolly George Varghese* (1980) | Municipal law prevails in a conflict with a treaty |

## Exam traps

- Art. 38(1) is **not** a hierarchy — do not rank treaties above custom.
- UNGA resolutions are **not** a source in Art. 38(1) — they are evidence of *opinio juris*.
- ICJ decisions bind **only the parties** (Art. 59) — they are not precedent in the common-law sense.
- The persistent objector is **not bound**; the State that merely stays silent is bound.
- *Lotus* = freedom unless prohibited; *North Sea* = two-element custom test — do not swap them.
- *Uniting for Peace* Resolution 377 (V), 1950 — **PYQ: Jun 2023, Dec 2023, Dec 2025**.
