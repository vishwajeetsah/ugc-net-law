# Cheat Sheet — The Law of Treaties (VCLT 1969)

One-page revision sheet for Unit III. Article numbers are the exam's favourite matching-set material — learn each number with its rule.

> Study note — legal information for exam preparation, not legal advice.

## VCLT at a glance

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Adopted / in force | Opened for signature 23 May 1969; in force **27 January 1980** |
| Parties | 119 States parties (2026) |
| Scope (Art. 1) | Treaties **between States** only |
| India | **Neither signatory nor party** — follows it as custom (MEA SoP; *AWAS Ireland*, Delhi HC, 2015) |

## The article map

| Art. | Rule | One line |
|---|---|---|
| 2(1)(a) | Definition of treaty | Written + between States + governed by international law, whatever its name |
| 2(1)(d) | Reservation | Unilateral statement excluding/modifying legal effect of provisions |
| 6 | Capacity | Every State may conclude treaties |
| 7(2)(a) | Full powers | Heads of State/Government, Foreign Ministers need none |
| 9(2) | Adoption | Two-thirds of States present and voting at a conference |
| 11–15 | Consent to be bound | Signature / exchange / ratification / acceptance / approval / accession |
| 18 | Interim obligation | Do not defeat the **object and purpose** before entry into force |
| 19 | Reservations permitted | Unless prohibited, not among specified ones, or **incompatible with object and purpose** |
| 20(5) | 12-month rule | Silence for 12 months = acceptance of a reservation |
| 21 | Effects of reservations | Modifies provisions **reciprocally**; objection without "definitely" = treaty still in force between the two |
| 24 | Entry into force | As the treaty provides; otherwise when all negotiators consent |
| 26 | **Pacta sunt servanda** | Treaties in force bind and must be performed **in good faith** |
| 27 | Internal law no excuse | May not invoke internal law for failure to perform (except Art. 46) |
| 28 | Non-retroactivity | No binding for acts before entry into force, unless intended |
| 30 | Successive treaties | Later treaty prevails between parties to both (Charter Art. 103 prevails over all) |
| 31 | Interpretation | Good faith + **ordinary meaning** + context + object and purpose |
| 32 | Supplementary means | *Travaux préparatoires* — to **confirm**, or where ambiguous/absurd |
| 33 | Multilingual texts | Equally authoritative; reconcile to the meaning fitting object and purpose |
| 34 | Third States | No obligations or rights for a third State without its consent |
| 35 | Third-State obligations | Need intent + **express written acceptance** |
| 36 | Third-State rights | Need intent; assent **presumed** until contrary indication |
| 39–41 | Amendment / modification | By agreement; notify all parties (40); inter se modification only if permitted/not prohibited (41) |
| 46 | Internal-law invalidity | Only for **manifest** violation of a rule of **fundamental importance** on competence |
| 48 | Error | Only for a fact/situation assumed to exist, forming the **essential basis** of consent |
| 51 | Coercion of representative | Consent has **no legal effect** |
| 52 | Coercion of a State | Treaty procured by threat/use of force is **void** |
| 53 | *Jus cogens* | Treaty conflicting with a peremptory norm at conclusion is **void** |
| 56 | Denunciation if treaty silent | **Not implied** unless intended or implied by nature; 12 months' notice |
| 60 | Material breach | Repudiation, or violation of a provision **essential to the object or purpose** |
| 61 | Supervening impossibility | Object indispensable for execution permanently gone; temporary = suspension only |
| 62 | *Rebus sic stantibus* | Essential basis + radically transforms obligations; **boundary treaties excluded** |
| 64 | New *jus cogens* | Conflicting treaty becomes void and terminates |
| 65 | Procedure | Notify claim + reasons; **3 months** without objection → may act |
| 66 | Disputes | Jus cogens disputes → **ICJ**; others → conciliation |
| 80 + Charter 102 | Registration | Register with UN Secretariat; unregistered treaty cannot be invoked before a UN organ |
| Charter 103 | Charter primacy | Charter obligations prevail over any other treaty |

## Is it a treaty? — the intention test

| Case | Instrument | Result |
|---|---|---|
| *Aegean Sea* (ICJ, 1978) | Joint communiqué | **Not** a treaty — no binding intent |
| *Qatar v. Bahrain* (ICJ, 1994) | Signed Doha Minutes | **Treaty** — "the parties commit themselves" |
| *Nuclear Tests* (ICJ, 1974) | Unilateral French declarations | **Binding** — public declaration with intent to be bound |

## The three hard articles — what the Court actually did

| Case | Articles tested | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| *Reservations to the Genocide Convention* (ICJ, 1951) | Reservation / object and purpose | Compatible reservation allowed even over objections |
| *Fisheries Jurisdiction* (UK v. Iceland, ICJ, 1974) | Art. 62 | Rebus sic stantibus **rejected**; 1961 agreement binding |
| *Gabcikovo-Nagymaros* (Hungary v. Slovakia, ICJ, 1997) | Arts. 60–62 | Treaty **remained in force**; Arts. 61/62 not established; Variant C a breach |

## Confused pairs

| Pair | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Art. 26 vs Art. 27 | 26 = perform in good faith; 27 = cannot plead internal law |
| Art. 31 vs Art. 32 | 31 = primary (ordinary meaning); 32 = **supplementary** (travaux to confirm) |
| Art. 51 vs Art. 52 | 51 = coercion of a **representative** (no legal effect); 52 = coercion of a **State** (void) |
| Art. 53 vs Art. 64 | 53 = jus cogens at **conclusion** (void); 64 = **new** jus cogens (void and terminates) |
| Void vs voidable | Arts. 51–53 = **void**; Arts. 46–50 = **voidable** (may lose the right by acquiescence, Art. 45) |
| Art. 60 vs Art. 62 | 60 = other party's **breach**; 62 = **change of circumstances** (narrow, boundary excluded) |
| Art. 35 vs Art. 36 | 35 = third-State **obligation** (express written acceptance); 36 = third-State **right** (assent presumed) |
| Signature vs ratification | Signature binds only if the treaty says so (Art. 12); otherwise consent is by ratification (Art. 14) |

## Exam traps

- **India is NOT a party to the VCLT** — neither signed nor ratified; it follows the rules as custom.
- **Travaux are supplementary, never primary** — the ICJ starts with the text (Art. 31).
- **Boundary treaties can never be ended by rebus sic stantibus** — Art. 62(2)(a).
- **A State cannot rely on its own breach** to invoke impossibility or fundamental change (Arts. 61(2), 62(2)(b)) — the *Gabcikovo* point.
- **Silence = acceptance** for reservations after 12 months (Art. 20(5)) — but an objection does **not** by itself kill the treaty between the two States (Art. 20(4)(b)).
- **Art. 46 is the only exception to Art. 27** — and it needs a *manifest* violation of a rule of *fundamental importance*.
- **Denunciation is not implied** when a treaty is silent (Art. 56) — 12 months' notice if the right exists.
- **Registration matters** — an unregistered treaty cannot be invoked before any UN organ (Charter Art. 102(2)).
