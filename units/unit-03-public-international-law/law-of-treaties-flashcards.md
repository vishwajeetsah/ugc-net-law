# Flashcards — The Law of Treaties (VCLT 1969)

25 term → one-line cards for rapid revision of Unit III. Cover the card, recall the answer, then check.

> Study note — legal information for exam preparation, not legal advice.

| # | Term | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Treaty (Art. 2(1)(a)) | An international agreement concluded between States in written form and governed by international law, whatever its designation |
| 2 | VCLT adopted / in force | Opened for signature 23 May 1969; entered into force 27 January 1980 |
| 3 | VCLT scope (Art. 1) | Treaties between States only — agreements with international organisations fall under the 1986 Convention |
| 4 | India and the VCLT | Neither signatory nor party; follows its provisions in practice as customary international law |
| 5 | Full powers (Art. 7) | Authority to represent a State; Heads of State/Government and Foreign Ministers need none (Art. 7(2)(a)) |
| 6 | Adoption of a text (Art. 9(2)) | At an international conference, by two-thirds of the States present and voting |
| 7 | Consent to be bound (Art. 11) | By signature, exchange of instruments, ratification, acceptance, approval or accession |
| 8 | Interim obligation (Art. 18) | After signature or consent pending entry into force, refrain from acts defeating the object and purpose |
| 9 | Reservation (Art. 2(1)(d)) | Unilateral statement purporting to exclude or modify the legal effect of certain provisions |
| 10 | Reservation barred (Art. 19) | If prohibited by the treaty, not among specified reservations, or incompatible with the object and purpose |
| 11 | *Genocide Reservations* (ICJ, 1951) | A reservation compatible with the object and purpose is permitted even over objections — the object-and-purpose test |
| 12 | Twelve-month rule (Art. 20(5)) | A reservation is deemed accepted if no objection is raised within 12 months of notification |
| 13 | Effect of an objection (Art. 21) | Provisions under reservation do not apply between the two States to that extent; treaty still in force unless the objector definitely says otherwise |
| 14 | Pacta sunt servanda (Art. 26) | Every treaty in force is binding and must be performed in good faith |
| 15 | Internal law no excuse (Art. 27) | A party may not invoke internal law to justify failure to perform a treaty |
| 16 | Interpretation (Art. 31) | Good faith + ordinary meaning + context + object and purpose |
| 17 | Travaux préparatoires (Art. 32) | Supplementary means — to confirm the Art. 31 meaning, or where it is ambiguous or absurd |
| 18 | Multilingual treaties (Art. 33) | Each authenticated text is equally authoritative; reconcile to the meaning fitting the object and purpose |
| 19 | Third States (Art. 34) | A treaty creates no obligations or rights for a third State without its consent (*pacta tertiis*) |
| 20 | Third-State right (Art. 36) | Needs the parties' intent; the third State's assent is presumed until the contrary is indicated |
| 21 | Invalidity — internal law (Art. 46) | Only for a manifest violation of a rule of internal law of fundamental importance on competence |
| 22 | Coercion of a State (Art. 52) | A treaty procured by threat or use of force is void |
| 23 | Jus cogens (Art. 53) | A treaty conflicting with a peremptory norm at conclusion is void; a new peremptory norm voids and terminates it (Art. 64) |
| 24 | Material breach (Art. 60) | Repudiation, or violation of a provision essential to the object or purpose of the treaty |
| 25 | Rebus sic stantibus (Art. 62) | Fundamental change of circumstances — essential basis of consent + radically transforms obligations; boundary treaties excluded |
| 26 | Supervening impossibility (Art. 61) | Permanent disappearance/destruction of an object indispensable for execution; temporary = suspension only |
| 27 | Termination procedure (Art. 65) | Notify claim and reasons; 3 months without objection → may carry out the measure |
| 28 | *Gabcikovo-Nagymaros* (ICJ, 1997) | Treaty remained in force; Arts. 60–62 all failed; parties to negotiate in good faith |
| 29 | *Qatar v. Bahrain* (ICJ, 1994) | Signed minutes (Doha Minutes) held to be a binding treaty — intention decides |
| 30 | *Aegean Sea* (ICJ, 1978) | Joint communiqué held NOT a treaty — no intention to create binding obligations |
| 31 | Registration (Charter Art. 102) | Treaties must be registered with the UN Secretariat; an unregistered treaty cannot be invoked before a UN organ |
| 32 | Charter primacy (Charter Art. 103) | Charter obligations prevail over obligations under any other treaty |
