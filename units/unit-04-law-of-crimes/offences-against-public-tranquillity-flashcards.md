# Flashcards — Offences against Public Tranquillity (BNS ss. 189–197)

25 term → one-line-answer cards for Chapter XI, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (IPC Chapter VIII, ss. 141–160). (UGC NET Law, Code 58, Unit IV.)

| # | Term | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unlawful assembly — minimum persons | Five or more persons with a common object (BNS s. 189(1)). |
| 2 | The five common objects, s. 189(1) | (a) Overawe Government/public servant by criminal force; (b) resist execution of law/legal process; (c) commit mischief, criminal trespass or any offence; (d) by criminal force take property or enforce a right; (e) by criminal force compel a person to do/omit an act. |
| 3 | Lawful assembly becoming unlawful | Yes — the Explanation to s. 189(1): an assembly not unlawful when it assembled may subsequently become unlawful. |
| 4 | Punishment for being a member of an unlawful assembly | Up to 6 months, or fine, or both — s. 189(2) (IPC ss. 142–143). |
| 5 | Joining after a dispersal order | Up to 2 years, or fine, or both — s. 189(3) (IPC s. 145). |
| 6 | Armed member of an unlawful assembly | Up to 2 years, or fine, or both — s. 189(4) (IPC s. 144). |
| 7 | s. 189(5) — the trap | An assembly of 5+ persons *likely to cause a disturbance of the public peace* after a dispersal order — 6 months; it need NOT be an unlawful assembly (IPC s. 151). |
| 8 | Hiring persons for an unlawful assembly | Punishable as a member, and for any offence the hired person commits as a member — s. 189(6) (IPC s. 150). |
| 9 | Harbouring hired persons | Up to 6 months, or fine, or both — s. 189(7) (IPC s. 157). |
| 10 | Being hired / going armed when hired | Being hired: 6 months — s. 189(8); hired and armed: 2 years — s. 189(9) (IPC s. 158). |
| 11 | s. 190 — common-object liability | Every member at the time of the offence is guilty of any offence committed in prosecution of the common object, or known to be likely — IPC s. 149. |
| 12 | *Lalji v. State of U.P.* (1989) | The common object may develop on the spur of the moment; it need not be pre-planned. |
| 13 | *Masalti v. State of U.P.* (1965) | Mere presence in an unlawful assembly is not enough — membership sharing the common object must be proved. |
| 14 | Rioting — definition | Force or violence used by an unlawful assembly, or any member of it, in prosecution of the common object — s. 191(1). |
| 15 | Rioting — punishment | Up to 2 years — s. 191(2); **up to 5 years if armed** — s. 191(3) (BNS change; IPC s. 148 was 3 years). |
| 16 | s. 192 — provocation to riot | Malignantly or wantonly, by an illegal act, giving provocation intending/knowing rioting is likely: riot committed — 1 year; riot not committed — 6 months. |
| 17 | s. 193 — owner/occupier liability | Fine up to ₹1,000 only (an omission offence); IPC ss. 154–156 consolidated. |
| 18 | Affray — definition and punishment | Two or more persons fighting in a public place, disturbing the public peace — s. 194(1); up to 1 month, or fine up to ₹1,000, or both — s. 194(2). |
| 19 | Riot vs affray | Riot: 5+ persons, unlawful assembly, common object, force. Affray: 2+ persons, fighting in a public place, no common object needed. |
| 20 | s. 195(1) — obstructing a public servant | Assaulting/obstructing a public servant dispersing an unlawful assembly or suppressing a riot/affray: up to 3 years + fine **not less than ₹25,000** (new minimum fine). |
| 21 | s. 195(2) — threat or attempt | Threatening to assault or attempting to obstruct the public servant: up to 1 year, or fine, or both. |
| 22 | s. 196 — promoting enmity | Up to 3 years; **5 years in a place of worship** — s. 196(2); electronic communication expressly included. |
| 23 | *Bilal Ahmed Kaloo* (1997) | s. 153A (now s. 196) requires the intention to promote enmity — merely offensive words are not enough. |
| 24 | s. 197 — national integration | Up to 3 years; 5 years in a place of worship; **new s. 197(1)(d)** — false or misleading information jeopardising sovereignty, unity and integrity or security of India. |
| 25 | *Ram Manohar Lohia* (1966) | Three concentric circles: law and order (largest) > public order (public peace, safety and tranquillity; the even tempo of community life) > security of the State (smallest). |

**Deleted in the BNS:** IPC s. 153AA (carrying arms in any procession, or organising mass drill/training with arms) — never implemented, dropped.

**Preventive companion:** BNSS s. 163 (CrPC s. 144) — District Magistrate's order, max 2 months, extendable to 6 months by the State Government; *Babulal Parate* (1961), *Madhu Limaye* (1971), *Ramlila Maidan* (2012).
