# Cheat Sheet — Criminal Intimidation, Insult and Defamation (BNS ss. 351–357)

One-page revision sheet for Unit IV, Chapter XIX of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Companion to `criminal-intimidation-insult-defamation.md`.

## Three wrongs, three targets

| Section | Works on | Core element |
|---|---|---|
| s. 351 | **Fear** | Threat of injury to person / reputation / property + intent to cause alarm or compel an act or omission |
| s. 352 | **Temper** | Intentional insult + provocation + intent/knowledge it will break the public peace |
| s. 356 | **Reputation** | Imputation, made or published, intending/knowing it will harm reputation |

## Defamation — the ten Exceptions (s. 356(1))

1. **Truth + public good** — truth alone is NOT enough
2. Public servant's conduct (good faith)
3. Public question (good faith)
4. Substantially true court report
5. Merits of a case decided by a Court (good faith)
6. Performance submitted to the public (good faith)
7. Censure by lawful authority (good faith)
8. Accusation to lawful authority (good faith)
9. Imputation for protection of interests / public good (good faith)
10. Caution in good faith

Plus 4 Explanations: deceased person (family's feelings); company / association; alternative or ironic imputation; what "lowers reputation" means.

## BNS changes to remember

- **Community service** is new — and only in **ss. 355 and 356(2)** in this chapter (not for the printer/seller under 356(3)–(4)).
- s. 353 now covers statements **"including through electronic means"**.
- IPC s. 509 (insulting modesty of a woman) moved out — it is now **BNS s. 79** (Chapter V).
- Chapter XIX consolidates IPC Chapters XIX + XXI + XXII into ss. 351–357.

## Case law — one line each

| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| *Subramanian Swamy v. UOI* (2016) | Criminal defamation **constitutional**; reputation is Article 21; "defamation" in Art. 19(2) covers criminal defamation |
| *Bilal Ahmed Kaloo v. State of A.P.* (1997) | s. 353(2) needs **mens rea** — intent to promote enmity; mere criticism is not an offence |
| *Manzar Sayeed Khan v. State of Maharashtra* (2007) | Scholarly/historical writing is not s. 353(2) without intent to promote enmity |
| *Amish Devgan v. UOI* (2021) | Free speech vs hate speech line; s. 153A upheld — frames s. 353(2) |
| *Manik Taneja v. State of Karnataka* (2015) | Abuse without a threat of injury is **not** criminal intimidation |
| *Vikram Johar v. State of U.P.* (2019) | The threat must be **real** and intended to cause alarm |
| *M.A. Rumugam v. Kittu* (2009) | "Publication" needs communication to a **third person** |
| *Chaman Lal v. State of Punjab* (1970) | Exception 9: accused must show **good faith**; burden on the accused |
| *R. Rajagopal v. State of T.N.* (1994) | Public officials have no privacy in **public records** |
| *Pravasi Bhalai Sangathan v. UOI* (2014) | Court declined to frame new hate-speech law; Law Commission 267th Report (2017) followed |

## Exam traps

- **s. 353(1)(b) vs s. 353(2):** (1)(b) = fear/alarm inducing an offence against the State/public tranquillity — **need not be false**. (2) = **false** information/rumour/alarming news creating enmity — **must be false**.
- **s. 353(2) vs s. 196:** both punish promoting enmity; s. 353(2) needs **false information** — s. 196 does not.
- **s. 353(2) vs s. 197(1)(d):** (2) = false info creating enmity **between groups**; 197(1)(d) = false/misleading info jeopardising **sovereignty, unity and integrity or security**.
- **s. 354 vs s. 351:** Divine displeasure is not "injury" — separate offence, lighter punishment (1 yr). Dharna illustration.
- **s. 351(4) stacks:** anonymous communication adds up to 2 years **on top of** the 2- or 7-year term.
- **Exception 1 trap:** truth **and** public good — both required.
- **Publication trap:** imputation sent only to the person concerned is not publication.
- **Defamation procedure:** only on the **aggrieved person's complaint** (BNSS s. 222); non-cognizable, bailable, compoundable. Public Prosecutor route only for high constitutional functionaries, with State Government sanction.
- **Shortest punishment in the chapter:** s. 355 — 24 hours' simple imprisonment.
