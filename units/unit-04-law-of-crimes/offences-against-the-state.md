# Unit IV — Law of Crimes: Offences against the State (BNS ss. 147–158)

Revision note. Chapter VII of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (ss. 147–158) carries the offences against the State — the old Chapter VI of the IPC (ss. 121–130). The structure is unchanged in substance, but one change is examinable above all others: **IPC s. 124A (sedition) is deleted, and BNS s. 152 takes its place with a narrower, differently-framed offence.**

> **PYQ hook:** s. 152 was asked in the very first BNS cycle — June 2025. The IPC → BNS mapping of this chapter (121 → 147, 121A → 148, 122 → 149, 123 → 150, 124 → 151, 124A → deleted / 152, 125 → 153, 126 → 154, 127 → 155, 128 → 156, 129 → 157, 130 → 158) is a ready-made matching set.

## 1. The chapter at a glance

| BNS | IPC | Offence | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| s. 147 | s. 121 | Waging war against the Government of India, or attempting / abetting it | Death, or imprisonment for life + fine |
| s. 148 | s. 121A | Conspiracy to commit offences punishable by s. 147 | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 149 | s. 122 | Collecting arms, etc., with intention of waging war | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 150 | s. 123 | Concealing with intent to facilitate a design to wage war | Up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 151 | s. 124 | Assaulting the President, Governor, etc., with intent to compel or restrain the exercise of lawful power | Up to 7 years + fine |
| s. 152 | — (replaces s. 124A) | Acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity of India | Life, or up to 7 years + fine |
| s. 153 | s. 125 | Waging war against the Government of any foreign State at peace with India | Life, or up to 7 years + fine |
| s. 154 | s. 126 | Committing depredation on the territories of any foreign State at peace with India | Up to 7 years + fine + forfeiture of property used |
| s. 155 | s. 127 | Receiving property taken by war or depredation mentioned in ss. 153–154 | Up to 7 years + fine + forfeiture of property |
| s. 156 | s. 128 | Public servant voluntarily allowing a prisoner of State or war to escape | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 157 | s. 129 | Public servant negligently suffering such a prisoner to escape | Simple imprisonment up to 3 years + fine |
| s. 158 | s. 130 | Aiding the escape of, rescuing or harbouring such a prisoner | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |

**Two patterns to notice for MCQs:** (i) the offences are ordered by gravity — waging war first, then its conspiracy, preparation and concealment, then the assault on constitutional heads, then the new s. 152, then foreign-State offences, then prisoner-of-war offences; (ii) only s. 147 carries death; only s. 157 is punishable with simple imprisonment alone.

## 2. Waging war — s. 147 (IPC s. 121)

"Whoever wages war against the Government of India, or attempts to wage such war, or abets the waging of such war, shall be punished with death, or imprisonment for life and shall also be liable to fine."

- **"Government of India"** means the Central Government — not a State Government. War against a State Government is not s. 147 (it may be other offences, e.g. rioting or s. 152).
- **What counts as waging war:** it is not ordinary rioting or a violent protest. The act must be in the nature of war — an attempt to attain an object of a general public nature by armed force, e.g. an organised armed insurrection aimed at overthrowing or intimidating the Government. A riot to settle a private score, however violent, is not waging war.
- **Attempt and abetment are punished on par with the completed offence** — the same maximum (death/life). This is unusual; most inchoate offences are punished less severely (cf. attempt, BNS s. 62, capped at half the longest term).
- **Case law:** *State (NCT of Delhi) v. Navjot Sandhu* (2005) — the Parliament attack case; the Supreme Court upheld convictions for waging war (IPC s. 121) and conspiracy (s. 121A) where the attack was directed at the seat of Government itself. The case is the standard modern authority on the scope of s. 121/147.
- **Sanction:** prosecution for offences under this chapter needs the previous sanction of the Central Government (BNSS s. 222; earlier CrPC s. 196) — see §6 below.

## 3. The cluster around waging war — ss. 148–151

- **s. 148 (IPC s. 121A) — conspiracy:** conspiring to commit an offence punishable by s. 147, or to overawe the Central or a State Government by criminal force or the show of criminal force. Note the second limb reaches further than s. 147 — "overawing" a Government by criminal force is punishable even where no war is waged.
- **s. 149 (IPC s. 122) — preparation:** collecting men, arms or ammunition, or otherwise preparing to wage war against the Government of India. One of the few places where *preparation* is itself a substantive offence (cf. preparation to commit dacoity, BNS s. 310(4)).
- **s. 150 (IPC s. 123) — concealment:** concealing the existence of a design to wage war, intending by that concealment to facilitate waging war. Mere knowledge is not enough — there must be intent to facilitate.
- **s. 151 (IPC s. 124) — assault on constitutional heads:** assaulting, wrongfully restraining, or attempting to assault or wrongfully restrain the President of India or the Governor of a State, with intent to compel or restrain the exercise of any lawful power. The offence protects the *office and its lawful functions*, not the person as such.

## 4. Section 152 — the sedition replacement (the most important section in this chapter)

**Text (substance):** whoever, purposely or knowingly, by words (spoken or written), signs, visible representation, electronic communication, or by use of financial resources, or otherwise, excites or attempts to excite **secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities**, or encourages feelings of **separatist activities**, or **endangers the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India**, shall be punished with imprisonment for life, or imprisonment up to 7 years, and fine.

**How s. 152 differs from the deleted IPC s. 124A:**

| | IPC s. 124A (sedition, deleted) | BNS s. 152 |
|---|---|---|
| Core wrong | Bringing or attempting to bring into hatred or contempt, or exciting or attempting to excite disaffection towards, the Government established by law | Exciting secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities; encouraging separatist feelings; endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity |
| Mental element | Intention (implied by the words used) | "Purposely or knowingly" — express |
| Target | The *Government* (disaffection towards it) | The *sovereignty, unity and integrity of India* |
| Means | Words, signs, visible representation | Words, signs, visible representation, **electronic communication, use of financial resources** |
| Punishment | Life, or up to 3 years + fine | Life, or up to 7 years + fine |
| Explanation protecting comment | Explanations 2–3: comments expressing disapprobation of Government measures, without exciting hatred/contempt/disaffection, are not offences | No equivalent explanation — but the narrower target (secession/armed rebellion/sovereignty) itself excludes mere criticism of the Government |

**Three consequences to remember:**
1. **Criticism of the Government is not s. 152.** Under s. 124A the charge was "disaffection towards the Government"; under s. 152 the conduct must be directed at secession, armed rebellion or the sovereignty/unity/integrity of India. Strong criticism, even hostile criticism, of government policy does not satisfy the section.
2. **The means are modernised:** electronic communication and the use of financial resources are expressly included — a funding channel for separatist activity falls within the section.
3. **The punishment ceiling rises** from 3 years (124A) to 7 years (152), while life remains the maximum.

## 5. From *Kedar Nath Singh* to *Vombatkere* — the sedition story (exam favourite)

The constitutionality of s. 124A was the subject of a 60-year arc that the BNS now closes:

- **1950 — *Romesh Thappar v. State of Madras*:** the Supreme Court distinguished "security of the State" (the Art. 19(2) ground that alone can support a sedition law) from "public order". Only the gravest offences — rebellion, waging war, insurrection — endanger the security of the State.
- **1962 — *Kedar Nath Singh v. State of Bihar* (5-judge bench):** s. 124A was **upheld but read down**. Only acts that have the intention or tendency to create disorder, or disturbance of law and order, or incitement to violence, fall within it. Mere strong words, even disloyal ones, without that tendency are not sedition. (Full case brief: `case-brief-kedar-nath-singh-v-state-of-bihar.md`.)
- **1995 — *Balwant Singh v. State of Punjab*:** raising slogans ("Khalistan Zindabad") on the day of Indira Gandhi's assassination, without any incitement to violence, was held **not** to be sedition. Casual sloganeering is not the "tendency to create disorder" *Kedar Nath* requires.
- **2022 — *S.G. Vombatkere v. Union of India*:** the Supreme Court kept s. 124A **in abeyance** (11 May 2022) and directed the Union to reconsider the provision; no fresh FIRs were to be registered and pending cases were kept on hold.
- **2023–24 — the BNS:** Parliament deleted s. 124A and enacted s. 152 instead — the abeyance is now moot for new cases, but *Kedar Nath*'s reading-down logic (only conduct threatening public order / the State's integrity, not mere dissent) remains the lens through which s. 152 will be tested.

**PYQ hook:** expect questions pairing *Kedar Nath Singh* (1962) with s. 152 — "which case upheld sedition with a narrowed reading?" and "what did s. 152 replace?". Both are asked from this single story.

## 6. Foreign-State offences — ss. 153–155

- **s. 153 (IPC s. 125):** waging war against the Government of any foreign State at peace with India. The foreign State must be at peace with India; war against a State at war with India is not this offence.
- **s. 154 (IPC s. 126):** committing depredation on the territories of such a foreign State — plundering, raiding, seizing property on its territory.
- **s. 155 (IPC s. 127):** receiving property taken by war or depredation under ss. 153–154, knowing it to have been so taken. Note the **forfeiture** of the property in ss. 154–155 — one of the few places the BNS still uses forfeiture as a punishment (cf. s. 4).
- These sections protect India's foreign relations: private citizens must not drag India into conflict with a State at peace with it.

## 7. Prisoners of State or war — ss. 156–158

A **prisoner of State** is a person detained by the Government for reasons of State (e.g. a political detainee); a **prisoner of war** is a person captured in war.

- **s. 156 (IPC s. 128):** a public servant *voluntarily* allowing such a prisoner to escape — life or up to 10 years + fine.
- **s. 157 (IPC s. 129):** a public servant *negligently* suffering such a prisoner to escape — simple imprisonment up to 3 years + fine. The voluntary/negligent pair is the classic exam contrast: same result (escape), different mental element, very different punishment.
- **s. 158 (IPC s. 130):** *any person* (not only a public servant) aiding the escape of, rescuing or harbouring such a prisoner — life or up to 10 years + fine.

## 8. Sanction for prosecution — BNSS s. 222

No court may take cognizance of an offence under this chapter (and certain others) except with the **previous sanction of the Central Government** (BNSS s. 222, corresponding to CrPC s. 196). For offences under s. 152 the sanction requirement is a practical filter: the State cannot prosecute without the Centre's prior approval. Questions on this are usually framed as "which authority's sanction is required for offences against the State?" — answer: the Central Government.

## 9. Quick-revision checklist

- [ ] s. 147 = waging war against the **Government of India** — death or life; attempt/abetment punished the same.
- [ ] s. 148 conspiracy / s. 149 preparation / s. 150 concealment — the waging-war cluster, in descending gravity.
- [ ] s. 151 — assault on the President/Governor to compel or restrain lawful power — up to 7 years.
- [ ] s. 152 — **replaces s. 124A**; "purposely or knowingly"; secession, armed rebellion, subversive/separatist activities; endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity; life or up to 7 years.
- [ ] s. 153–155 — foreign State at peace: war / depredation / receiving property; forfeiture in ss. 154–155.
- [ ] s. 156 voluntary vs s. 157 negligent escape of prisoner of State/war (public servant); s. 158 aiding/rescuing/harbouring by anyone.
- [ ] *Kedar Nath Singh* (1962) — s. 124A upheld, read down to incitement to violence / tendency to public disorder.
- [ ] *Balwant Singh* (1995) — slogans alone, no sedition. *Vombatkere* (2022) — s. 124A kept in abeyance.
- [ ] Sanction: Central Government, BNSS s. 222 (CrPC s. 196).
