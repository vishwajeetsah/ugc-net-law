# Cheat Sheet — Hurt, Grievous Hurt and Assault (BNS ss. 114–136)

One-page revision sheet. IPC equivalents in brackets.

## 1. The ladder: hurt → grievous hurt

| Section | What | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| **s. 114** [319] | Hurt = bodily pain, disease or infirmity | — (definition) |
| **s. 115** [321+323] | Voluntarily causing hurt | Up to **1 yr** / fine ₹10,000 |
| **s. 116** [320] | Grievous hurt — 8 kinds (below) | — (definition) |
| **s. 117(2)** [322+325] | Voluntarily causing grievous hurt | Up to **7 yrs** + fine |
| **s. 117(3)** — NEW | Grievous hurt → permanent disability / persistent vegetative state | RI **min 10 yrs to life** |
| **s. 117(4)** — NEW | Group of **5+** acting in concert, identity ground, grievous hurt — each member | Up to **7 yrs** + fine |

## 2. The eight kinds of grievous hurt — s. 116 (memorise verbatim)

1. Emasculation · 2. Permanent privation of sight of either eye · 3. Permanent privation of hearing of either ear · 4. Privation of any member or joint · 5. Destruction / permanent impairing of powers of any member or joint · 6. Permanent disfiguration of head or face · 7. Fracture or dislocation of a bone or tooth · 8. Hurt endangering life, **or** severe bodily pain / unable to follow ordinary pursuits for **20 days**

## 5. Restraint and confinement — ss. 126–127

| Section | Test | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| **s. 126** [339] | Voluntarily obstructs a person from a direction he has a **right** to proceed | Up to **1 mo** / fine ₹5,000 |
| **s. 127(2)** [342] | Confinement within circumscribing limits | Up to **1 yr** / fine ₹5,000 |
| **s. 127(3)** [343] | Confinement **3+ days** | Up to **3 yrs** / fine ₹10,000 |
| **s. 127(4)** [344] | Confinement **10+ days** | Up to **5 yrs** + fine min ₹10,000 |
| **s. 127(5)** [345] | Writ for liberation issued | **+2 yrs** |
| **s. 127(6)** [346] | Secret confinement | **+3 yrs** |
| **s. 127(7)** [347] | To extort property / constrain to illegal act | **+3 yrs** |
| **s. 127(8)** [348] | To extort confession | **+3 yrs** |

Restraint blocks a **direction**; confinement blocks escape **beyond limits**. Every confinement is restraint; not every restraint is confinement.

## 6. Force, criminal force, assault — ss. 128–136

| Section | What |
|---|---|
| **s. 128** [349] | **Force** — causing motion / change of motion / cessation of motion to a person or a substance in contact with them, without consent, to commit an offence or intending injury, fear or annoyance |
| **s. 129** [350] | **Criminal force** — force used **intentionally**, without consent, to commit an offence or intending injury, fear or annoyance |
| **s. 130** [351] | **Assault** — gesture or preparation intending / knowing it likely to cause apprehension of **immediate** criminal force. Mere words are **not** assault (but words may give meaning to gestures) |

## 7. Cases to name-drop

- ***Laxmi v. Union of India* (2014)** — acid sale regulated; ₹3 lakh compensation.
- ***Parivartan Kendra v. Union of India* (2015)** — free treatment for acid victims.
- ***Tuberville v. Savage* (1669)** — conditional threat, no assault.
- ***Stephens v. Myers* (1830)** — advancing with clenched fist = assault.
- ***R v. Ireland* (1997)** — silent calls can be assault; apprehension is the test.
- ***State of Karnataka v. Sharanappa* (2002)** — whether hurt is grievous is a question of fact on the evidence.

## 8. Traps in one line

- s. 124 = acid (both sub-sections); s. 125 = endangering life. Never swap.
- s. 117(3) = **result** (disability/vegetative, min 10 yrs–life); s. 117(4) = **group** (5+, up to 7 yrs).
- s. 103(2) = group **murder**; s. 117(4) = group **grievous hurt** — same structure, different offence.
- s. 122 (provocation) **reduces** punishment for hurt; it is not a defence.
- "Voluntarily" needs intention **or** knowledge the hurt was likely; for grievous hurt, the grievousness must also have been intended or known likely (Explanation, s. 117(1)).
