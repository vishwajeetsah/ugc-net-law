# Unit IV — Law of Crimes: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

Revision note. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) replaced the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC) with effect from **1 July 2024**. Since the June 2025 cycle the paper tests the BNS alongside the IPC — both codes must be studied, and the IPC → BNS section mapping is a favourite matching-set format.

> **Transition rule (BNS s. 358):** the IPC stands repealed, but s. 358(2) saves it for offences committed **before 1 July 2024**. An offence committed on 30 June 2024 is still tried under the IPC; one committed on 1 July 2024 is tried under the BNS.

## 1. The new code at a glance

| | IPC, 1860 | BNS, 2023 |
|---|---|---|
| Sections | 511 | 358 |
| Chapters | 23 | 20 |
| In force | 1 Jan 1862 | 1 Jul 2024 |
| Companion codes | CrPC, 1973; Evidence Act, 1872 | BNSS, 2023; BSA, 2023 |

The BNS consolidates, renumbers and reorders the IPC rather than rewriting criminal law from scratch: most definitions and offences survive with new numbers, a few offences are new, and two colonial-era provisions are gone. The syllabus still calls the unit "Law of Crimes" — the general principles (actus reus, mens rea, stages of crime, general exceptions) are unchanged in substance.

**PYQ hook:** the first clear BNS questions appeared in June 2025 — s. 152 (acts endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity) and s. 2(14) ("injury"). Mens rea and the s. 300 IPC exceptions (now BNS s. 101) recur in Dec 2023, Aug 2024, Jun 2025 and Dec 2025.

## 2. General principles

- **Actus reus + mens rea:** criminal liability still requires a prohibited act (or omission, where a legal duty to act exists) accompanied by a guilty mind. The BNS, like the IPC, defines the mental element offence-by-offence — "dishonestly" (s. 2(7)), "fraudulently" (s. 2(9)), "good faith" (s. 2(11)), "voluntarily" — rather than by one general definition.
- **Stages of crime:** intention → preparation (generally not punishable) → attempt (s. 62) → commission. Preparation is punishable only where the Code expressly says so — e.g. preparation to wage war (s. 149), preparation to commit dacoity (s. 310(4)).
- **Definitions (s. 2):** the BNS consolidates the IPC's scattered definitions into one section. New or modernised ones: s. 2(3) "child" (a person below 18 years); s. 2(10) "gender" (the pronoun "he" and its derivatives are used for any person, whether male, female or transgender); s. 2(14) "injury" (any harm illegally caused to any person, in body, mind, reputation or property).
- **Punishments (s. 4):** death; imprisonment for life; imprisonment (rigorous or simple); forfeiture of property; fine; and — new — **community service**. Community service is available for a handful of lighter offences, e.g. theft of property below ₹5,000 by a first-time offender (s. 303(2) proviso — only if the property is returned or its value paid), and in defamation (s. 356).

## 3. General exceptions (Chapter III, ss. 14–44)

The IPC's Chapter IV (ss. 76–106) is now Chapter III (ss. 14–44). The substance is unchanged.

|---|---|---|
| s. 14 | s. 76 | Act done by a person bound, or by mistake of fact believing himself bound, by law |
| s. 20 | s. 81 | Act likely to cause harm, done without criminal intent and in good faith to prevent other harm (necessity) |
| s. 21 | s. 82 | Act of a child under 7 years (*doli incapax*) |
| s. 22 | s. 83 | Act of a child above 7 and under 12 who has not attained sufficient maturity of understanding |
| s. 23 | s. 84 | Act of a person of unsound mind (McNaughten rules) |

**PYQ hook:** the exceptions are tested as matching sets and through fact situations ("A, a child of 8, steals…" — s. 22 turns on maturity of understanding, not age alone).

## 4. Inchoate offences: abetment, conspiracy, attempt

| BNS | IPC | Offence |
|---|---|---|
| ss. 45–60 | ss. 107–120 | Abetment (instigation, conspiracy, intentional aiding); abettor punished as the offender where the offence is committed |
| s. 61 | ss. 120A–120B | Criminal conspiracy — agreement to do an illegal act, or a legal act by illegal means |
| s. 62 | s. 511 | Attempt — punishable with up to half the longest term for the offence attempted |

## 5. Offences against the State (Chapter VII, ss. 147–158)

- **s. 147** — waging war against the Government of India (IPC s. 121): death or life imprisonment + fine.
- **s. 152** — acts endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India: whoever, purposely or knowingly, by words (spoken or written), signs, visible representation, electronic communication or otherwise, excites or attempts to excite secession, armed rebellion or subversive activities, or encourages feelings of separatist activities, or endangers the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India — life imprisonment or up to 7 years + fine.
  - **This replaces IPC s. 124A (sedition), which the BNS deletes.** The difference matters: s. 124A punished "disaffection" towards the government; s. 152 requires purpose or knowledge directed at secession, armed rebellion or endangering sovereignty, unity and integrity.
- **s. 153** — waging war against the Government of any foreign State at peace with India (IPC s. 125).
- **s. 196** — promoting enmity between different groups on grounds of religion, race, place of birth, residence, language, etc. (IPC s. 153A).
- **s. 197** — imputations, assertions prejudicial to national integration (IPC s. 153B).

### 6.2 Culpable homicide and murder (ss. 100–106)

- **s. 100** — culpable homicide (IPC s. 299); **s. 101** — murder (IPC s. 300): the four clauses of s. 300 are reproduced — intention to cause death; intention to cause bodily injury known to be likely to cause death; intention to cause bodily injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death; knowledge that the act is so imminently dangerous that it must in all probability cause death.
- **Exceptions to s. 101** (the five IPC s. 300 exceptions, unchanged): grave and sudden provocation; private defence; public servant acting in good faith exceeding powers; sudden fight; death with consent of the victim (above 18 years). Where an exception applies, the offence is culpable homicide not amounting to murder.
- **s. 103** — punishment for murder (IPC s. 302): death or life imprisonment + fine.
- **s. 103(2)** — **new:** murder by a group of five or more persons acting in concert on grounds of race, caste or community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief (mob lynching): death or life imprisonment + fine.
- **s. 104** — punishment for murder by a person under sentence of imprisonment for life (IPC s. 303): death or life imprisonment, life meaning the remainder of natural life. The mandatory death sentence of IPC s. 303 was struck down in *Mithu v. State of Punjab* (1983).
- **s. 105** — punishment for culpable homicide not amounting to murder (IPC s. 304): life or up to 10 years + fine (with intent); up to 10 years + fine (without intent).
- **s. 106** — causing death by negligence (IPC s. 304A): (1) up to 5 years + fine (raised from 2 years under the IPC); (2) rash or negligent driving causing death, where the driver escapes without reporting — up to 10 years + fine. **Note:** enforcement of s. 106(2) was put on hold by the government in January 2024, before the Code came into force, pending consultation — the provision is on the statute book but its operation was deferred.
- **s. 108** — abetment of suicide (IPC s. 306): up to 10 years + fine. **s. 109** — attempt to murder (IPC s. 307). **s. 110** — attempt to commit culpable homicide (IPC s. 308).

### 6.3 New organised-crime and terror provisions

| BNS | Offence / punishment |
|---|---|
| **s. 111** | **Organised crime** (new): continuing unlawful activity by a syndicate — death or life imprisonment where death is caused; otherwise 5 years to life + fine of at least ₹5 lakh |
| **s. 112** | **Petty organised crime** (new): theft, snatching, cheating, selling of public examination question papers and similar offences committed by a gang — 1 to 7 years + fine |
| **s. 113** | **Terrorist act** (new): acts threatening the unity, integrity, security or sovereignty of India, or striking terror — death or life imprisonment where death results; otherwise 5 years to life + fine |

### 6.4 Hurt, restraint, kidnapping, trafficking

- **Hurt / grievous hurt:** ss. 114–125 (IPC ss. 319–326B, 336–338). **s. 124** — voluntarily causing grievous hurt by use of acid (IPC ss. 326A–326B): s. 124(1) grievous hurt by acid — minimum 10 years to life + fine; s. 124(2) throwing or attempting to throw acid — 5 to 7 years + fine. **s. 125** — act endangering life or personal safety of others (IPC ss. 336–338).
- **Wrongful restraint / confinement:** ss. 126–127 (IPC ss. 339–342).
- **Kidnapping / abduction:** ss. 137–142 (IPC ss. 359–369). **s. 140** — kidnapping or abduction for ransom (IPC s. 364A): death or life imprisonment.
- **s. 143** — trafficking of person (IPC s. 370); **s. 144** — exploitation of a trafficked person (IPC s. 370A).
- **Unlawful assembly and rioting:** ss. 189–197 (IPC ss. 141–160). **s. 189** — unlawful assembly (IPC s. 141); **s. 191** — rioting (IPC s. 146): up to 2 years, or up to 5 years where armed with a deadly weapon (raised from 3 years under IPC s. 148).

## 8. What the BNS dropped

- **IPC s. 124A (sedition)** — deleted; replaced by the narrower s. 152 (see §5).
- **IPC s. 377 (unnatural offences)** — omitted. Consensual sexual acts between adults remain lawful after *Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India* (2018). A much-discussed gap follows: s. 63 defines rape only against a woman, so non-consensual sexual acts between men have no express rape provision under the BNS and must be pursued, if at all, through the hurt and assault provisions.
- **IPC s. 497 (adultery)** — not revived; it was struck down in *Joseph Shine v. Union of India* (2018).

## 9. Companion codes — one-line orientation

The BNS did not arrive alone. The **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** replaced the CrPC, 1973, and the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)** replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872 — all three in force from 1 July 2024. Unit IV questions occasionally cross into procedure and evidence (e.g. BNSS s. 479, the successor to CrPC s. 436A on release of undertrials — see the BailSetu project), but the substantive offences are all in the BNS.

## 10. Quick-revision checklist

- [ ] BNS in force 1 July 2024; IPC saved for pre-1 July 2024 offences (s. 358).
- [ ] Punishments, s. 4 — community service is the new sixth punishment.
- [ ] General exceptions, ss. 14–44 (IPC ss. 76–106): child under 7 (s. 21), unsound mind (s. 23), private defence (ss. 34–44).
- [ ] s. 152 replaces s. 124A — purpose/knowledge + secession, armed rebellion or sovereignty; no "disaffection".
- [ ] Sexual offences ch. V: s. 63 rape; s. 65(2) under-12 rape — death possible; **s. 69 deceitful promise of marriage — new**.
- [ ] Murder s. 101 / s. 103; **mob lynching s. 103(2) — new**; s. 104 murder by a life-convict; culpable homicide s. 105; negligence s. 106 (5 years; s. 106(2) hit-and-run 10 years — enforcement deferred).
- [ ] **Organised crime s. 111, petty organised crime s. 112, terrorist act s. 113 — all new.**
- [ ] Property: theft s. 303; **snatching s. 304 — new**; robbery s. 309; dacoity s. 310; cheating s. 318; criminal breach of trust s. 316 (5 years now).
- [ ] Dropped: s. 124A sedition, s. 377, s. 497 adultery.
