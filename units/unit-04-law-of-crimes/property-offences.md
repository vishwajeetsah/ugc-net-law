# Unit IV — Law of Crimes: Property offences (BNS ss. 303–334)

Revision note. Chapter XVII of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (ss. 303–334) carries the offences against property — the old Chapter XVII of the IPC (ss. 378–462). The BNS does two things at once here: it **consolidates** (85 IPC sections become 32 BNS sections, with definitions and graded punishments folded into single sections) and it **raises several punishments** (extortion, criminal breach of trust, mischief, criminal trespass fines). Both moves are examinable: expect matching questions on the new numbers and "what changed" questions on the punishments.

> **PYQ hook:** property offences are a perennial Paper 2 block — theft, dacoity, cheating and criminal breach of trust recur in almost every cycle, usually as definition questions, IPC → BNS mapping questions, and punishment questions. The new s. 304 (snatching) and the raised punishments (ss. 308, 314, 316, 324, 329) are the most likely fresh BNS questions from this chapter.

**The consolidation pattern to memorise:** where the IPC had one section per aggravated form, the BNS has one section with numbered sub-sections. That is the whole story of ss. 308, 310, 316, 317, 318, 324, 329 and 331. If a question asks for "the BNS section corresponding to IPC s. 406", the answer is s. 316(2) — not a freestanding section.

## 2. Theft — ss. 303–307

**s. 303(1) — the definition (IPC s. 378, same words).** Five elements, all required:

1. **Dishonest intention** — intention to cause wrongful gain to oneself or wrongful loss to another (BNS s. 2(7) defines "dishonestly").
2. **Movable property** — property that can be moved; land and things attached to the earth are excluded until severed.
3. **Out of the possession of any person** — the property must be in someone's possession; there is no theft of ownerless property.
4. **Without that person's consent** — consent obtained by deception is not real consent.
5. **Moving the property** — the offender "moves that property in order to such taking". The slightest movement completes the offence; asportation need not be far.

**Case law (the three theft cases that recur):**

- *K.N. Mehra v. State of Rajasthan* (1957) — dishonest intention is the gist of theft; taking for temporary use can still be theft if the intention is dishonest.
- *Pyare Lal Bhargava v. State of Rajasthan* (1963) — a government servant took a file home overnight to copy it and returned it. Held: theft. Temporary deprivation is enough; the intention at the time of taking decides, not the later return.
- *State of Kerala v. Mathai Verghese* (1986) — electricity is "movable property" and can be the subject of theft.

**s. 303(2) — punishment.** Up to 3 years, or fine, or both. Two BNS additions:

- **Repeat conviction:** on a second or subsequent conviction under s. 303, rigorous imprisonment of **not less than 1 year and up to 5 years**, plus fine.
- **Community service:** for a first offence where the value of the stolen property is below ₹5,000, the court may order community service instead — but only if the property is returned or its value paid (s. 303(2) proviso; community service is the new sixth punishment, BNS s. 4).

**Aggravated thefts (ss. 305–307)** — the place, the person, or the preparation aggravates:

- **s. 305 (IPC s. 380)** — theft in a dwelling house, or in a means of transportation, or in a place of worship: up to 7 years + fine.
- **s. 306 (IPC s. 381)** — theft by a clerk or servant of property in the possession of the master: up to 7 years + fine. The aggravation is the breach of the employment relationship — compare s. 316(4), CBT by a clerk or servant.
- **s. 307 (IPC s. 382)** — theft after preparation made for causing death, hurt or restraint in order to commit the theft: RI up to 10 years + fine. Mere preparation for the theft itself is not punishable; preparation *to cause harm* is.

## 3. Snatching — s. 304 (new offence)

"Whoever, in order to commit theft, suddenly or quickly or forcibly seizes or secures or grabs or takes away from any person or from his possession any movable property, shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term which may extend to three years, and shall also be liable to fine."

Three points for the exam:

1. **It is a form of theft, not of robbery.** The taking is sudden, quick or forcible — but the force is directed at the *property* (grabbing a chain, a phone, a bag), not at the *person*. If the offender voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt or wrongful restraint, or fear of instant death/hurt/restraint, the offence becomes **robbery** (s. 309).
2. **No decided case law yet** — the offence did not exist under the IPC. Questions can only test the definition and the distinction from theft and robbery.
3. **It also appears in s. 112** (petty organised crime): snatching committed by a gang is punishable with 1 to 7 years.

## 4. Extortion — s. 308 (IPC ss. 383–389 consolidated)

**s. 308(1) — the definition (IPC s. 383, same words).** Whoever *intentionally* puts any person in fear of any injury, and thereby *dishonestly induces* the person so put in fear to deliver property, a valuable security, or anything signed or sealed that may be converted into a valuable security, commits extortion. Two essentials: (i) fear of injury, (ii) dishonest inducement to deliver. A threat to publish a defamatory libel unless money is paid is extortion (Illustration (a)).

**The seven sub-sections — the ladder turns on two things: what was threatened, and whether the property was actually delivered.**

| Sub-section | IPC | Conduct | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 308(2) | 384 | Committing extortion | Up to **7 years**, or fine, or both |
| 308(3) | 385 | Putting / attempting to put in fear of *any injury*, in order to commit extortion | Up to 2 years, or fine, or both |
| 308(4) | 387 | Putting / attempting to put in fear of *death or grievous hurt*, in order to commit extortion | Up to 7 years + fine |
| 308(5) | 386 | *Committing* extortion by fear of death or grievous hurt | Up to 10 years + fine |
| 308(6) | 389 | Putting / attempting to put in fear of *accusation* of an offence punishable with death / life / 10 years, in order to commit extortion | Up to 10 years + fine |
| 308(7) | 388 | *Committing* extortion by threat of such an accusation | Up to 10 years + fine |

**Three exam points:**

1. **The big change: s. 308(2) is 7 years, not 3.** Under IPC s. 384 ordinary extortion carried up to 3 years; the BNS more than doubled it. This is the single most likely "what changed" question on this section.
2. **The order flipped.** In the IPC the *completed* offence came first (s. 386, fear of death/grievous hurt — 10 years) and the *preparatory* offence second (s. 387 — 7 years). In the BNS the preparatory offence comes first (308(4), 7 years) and the completed offence second (308(5), 10 years). So **308(4) = IPC 387 and 308(5) = IPC 386** — the punishments, not the order, identify the match.
3. **"In order to" vs "committing".** Sub-sections (3), (4) and (6) punish the *threat made in order to* extort (no delivery needed); (2), (5) and (7) punish the *completed* extortion. A threat alone is punishable — delivery is not an element of (3), (4) or (6).

## 5. Robbery and dacoity — ss. 309–313

**s. 309(1): "In all robbery there is either theft or robbery"** — robbery is not a freestanding offence; it is theft or extortion *aggravated by violence or the fear of instant violence*.

**When theft becomes robbery — s. 309(2).** In order to commit the theft, in committing it, or in carrying away (or attempting to carry away) the property obtained, the offender voluntarily causes or attempts to cause death, hurt or wrongful restraint, **or fear of instant death, hurt or wrongful restraint**. The word *instant* is the test: a threat of future harm ("your child will be killed unless you pay") is extortion, not robbery — unless the victim is put in fear of *instant* harm to the child (Illustration (d)).

**When extortion becomes robbery — s. 309(3).** The offender must be *in the presence* of the person put in fear, put that person in fear of *instant* death, hurt or wrongful restraint, and induce the person *then and there* to deliver the thing extorted. Presence means sufficiently near to put the other in fear of instant harm (Explanation).

**Punishments — s. 309(4)–(6):**

- (4) Robbery: RI up to 10 years + fine; if committed **on the highway between sunset and sunrise**, up to **14 years**.
- (5) Attempt to commit robbery: RI up to 7 years + fine.
- (6) Voluntarily causing hurt in committing or attempting robbery: **life, or RI up to 10 years** + fine — and the liability extends to any other person jointly concerned in the robbery.

**Dacoity — s. 310.** Robbery committed by **five or more persons conjointly**. The count includes persons present and aiding the commission or attempt — so four robbers plus one lookout = dacoity.

| Sub-section | IPC | Conduct | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 310(2) | 395 | Committing dacoity | Life, or RI up to 10 years + fine |
| 310(3) | 396 | Dacoity with murder (any one of the five commits murder) | Death, life, or RI **not less than 10 years** + fine — every one of the five is liable |
| 310(4) | 399 | Making preparation to commit dacoity | RI up to **10 years** + fine |
| 310(5) | 402 | Being one of five or more persons assembled for the purpose of committing dacoity | RI up to 7 years + fine |
| 310(6) | 400 | Belonging to a gang habitually committing dacoity | Life, or RI up to 10 years + fine |

**Watch the 310(4)/310(5) pair:** preparation is 10 years, assembling is 7 years. The two are the classic confused pair — and note that s. 310(4) is one of the rare places where *preparation* is itself a substantive offence (cf. ss. 149, 324(6)).

**Case law:** *Shyam Behari v. State of U.P.* (1957) — the "conjointly" requirement: all five must be concerned in the commission or attempt; a person merely present without aiding does not count toward the five.

**The two minimum-sentence sections:**

- **s. 311 (IPC s. 397)** — robbery or dacoity with attempt to cause death or grievous hurt: RI **not less than 7 years**.
- **s. 312 (IPC s. 398)** — attempt to commit robbery or dacoity when armed with a deadly weapon: RI **not less than 7 years**.
- **s. 313 (IPC s. 400)** — belonging to a gang associated in habitually committing *theft or robbery* (not dacoity — that is s. 310(6)): RI up to 7 years + fine.

## 6. Dishonest misappropriation and criminal breach of trust — ss. 314–316

**The distinction that frames this whole group:** in theft the dishonesty exists *at the taking* (the property is taken out of another's possession); in misappropriation and breach of trust the property comes into the person's possession *lawfully first*, and the dishonesty comes *later* — conversion to one's own use.

**s. 314 (IPC s. 403) — dishonest misappropriation of property.** Whoever dishonestly misappropriates or converts to his own use any movable property. The classic illustration: A finds a valuable ring, does not know the owner, and sells it at once without trying to discover the owner — guilty. But a person who takes property to protect it for the owner, or to restore it, does not misappropriate (Explanation 2). **BNS change: the punishment now runs from a minimum — not less than 6 months, up to 2 years, plus fine.** The IPC had no minimum.

**s. 315 (IPC s. 404) — misappropriation of property possessed by a deceased person at the time of death.** The property must not since have come into the possession of any person legally entitled to it. Punishment: up to 3 years + fine; if the offender was employed by the deceased as a clerk or servant, up to 7 years.

**s. 316 (IPC ss. 405–409 consolidated) — criminal breach of trust.** The definition (s. 316(1)) is the IPC s. 405 definition: a person entrusted with property, or with dominion over property, who dishonestly misappropriates or converts it to his own use, or dishonestly uses or disposes of it in violation of a direction of law or a legal contract, or wilfully suffers another person to do so, commits CBT. **Entrustment or dominion is the foundation** — without it there is no CBT, only misappropriation or theft.

| Sub-section | IPC | Who | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 316(2) | 406 | Anyone committing CBT | Up to **5 years**, or fine, or both |
| 316(3) | 407 | Carrier, wharfinger or warehouse-keeper | Up to 7 years + fine |
| 316(4) | 408 | Clerk or servant | Up to 7 years + fine |
| 316(5) | 409 | Public servant; banker, merchant, factor, broker, attorney or agent | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |

**The big change: s. 316(2) is 5 years, not 3.** IPC s. 406 capped general CBT at 3 years; the BNS raised it to 5.

**Case law:**

- *R.K. Dalmia v. Delhi Administration* (1962) — the leading authority on entrustment: entrustment need not be express, and "dominion over property" is enough; a person in control of property as an agent or employee can commit CBT even without a formal trust deed.
- *Sushil Suri v. CBI* (2011) — CBT vs cheating: CBT requires *entrustment* followed by dishonest misappropriation; cheating requires *deception* inducing delivery. Where there is no entrustment, the offence (if any) is cheating, not CBT.
- *Onkar Nath Mishra v. State (NCT of Delhi)* (2008) — the prosecution must prove entrustment *and* dishonest misappropriation; mere failure to account, without dishonest intent, is not CBT.

## 7. Stolen property — s. 317 (IPC ss. 410–414 consolidated)

**s. 317(1) — the definition (IPC s. 410).** Property is "stolen property" if its possession was transferred by theft, extortion, robbery, cheating, or criminal misappropriation, or in respect of which CBT was committed — whether within or outside India. It ceases to be stolen property once it comes into the possession of a person legally entitled to it.

| Sub-section | IPC | Conduct | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 317(2) | 411 | Dishonestly receiving or retaining stolen property, knowing / having reason to believe it is stolen | Up to 3 years, or fine, or both |
| 317(3) | 412 | Receiving / retaining property transferred by dacoity, or receiving from a person belonging to a gang of dacoits | Life, or RI up to 10 years + fine |
| 317(4) | 413 | Habitually receiving or dealing in stolen property | Life, or imprisonment up to 10 years + fine |
| 317(5) | 414 | Voluntarily assisting in concealing / disposing of / making away with stolen property | Up to 3 years, or fine, or both |

**The mental element is "knowing or having reason to believe"** — actual knowledge is not required; reason to believe suffices. But mere possession of property that happens to be stolen, without that mental element, is not an offence.

## 8. Cheating and fraudulent dealings — ss. 318–323

**s. 318(1) — the definition (IPC s. 415).** Whoever, by deceiving any person, fraudulently or dishonestly induces that person to deliver any property, or to consent to the retention of property, or intentionally induces that person to do or omit to do anything which he would not do or omit if he were not so deceived, and which act or omission causes or is likely to cause damage or harm to that person in body, mind, reputation or property, cheats. Two essentials: **deception** and **dishonest inducement** (to deliver property, or to act/omit to his harm).

**Punishments — s. 318:**

- (2) Cheating: up to **3 years**, or fine, or both.
- (4) Cheating and dishonestly inducing delivery of property (IPC s. 420): up to **7 years** + fine.

**s. 319 (IPC s. 416) — cheating by personation.** Pretending to be some other person, or falsely representing oneself to be some other person, or falsely representing another person to be oneself. Punishment: up to **5 years** + fine (IPC s. 419).

**Case law — the cheating / breach-of-contract line:**

- *Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v. State of Bihar* (2000) — the leading case: cheating requires a **dishonest intention at the inception** of the transaction. A mere failure to keep a promise, or a breach of contract, is not cheating without that initial dishonest intent.
- *State of Kerala v. A. Pareed Pillai* (1972) — deception alone is not cheating; there must also be dishonest inducement to deliver property or to act to one's harm.
- *S.W. Palanitkar v. State of Bihar* (2002) — reaffirms *Hridaya Ranjan*: civil liability and criminal cheating are distinct; the complaint must disclose dishonest intent from the start.

**Fraudulent dealings — ss. 320–323** (IPC ss. 421–424, punishments unchanged at up to 2 years, or fine, or both):

- **s. 320 (IPC s. 421)** — fraudulent removal or concealment of property to prevent its seizure as forfeited or in execution.
- **s. 321 (IPC s. 422)** — dishonestly or fraudulently preventing a debt from being available for creditors.
- **s. 322 (IPC s. 423)** — dishonest or fraudulent execution of a deed of transfer containing a false statement of consideration.
- **s. 323 (IPC s. 424)** — dishonest or fraudulent removal or concealment of property to prevent its distribution among creditors.

## 9. Mischief — ss. 324–328 (IPC ss. 425–440 consolidated)

The BNS collapsed sixteen IPC sections into five. The definition is unchanged; the punishments are now graded inside s. 324 itself.

**s. 324(1) — the definition (IPC s. 425).** Whoever, with intent to cause, or knowing that he is likely to cause, wrongful loss or damage to the public or to any person, causes the destruction of any property, or any such change in any property or in the situation thereof as destroys or diminishes its value or utility, or affects it injuriously, commits mischief. Essentials: (i) intention or knowledge of likely wrongful loss/damage; (ii) destruction, diminution of value/utility, or injurious change.

**s. 324(2)–(6) — the graded ladder (the IPC had separate sections for each of these):**

| Sub-section | IPC (nearest) | Conduct | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 324(2) | 426 | Mischief simpliciter | Up to **6 months**, or fine, or both |
| 324(3) | — | Mischief causing loss/damage to any property, including Government / Local Authority property | Up to 1 year, or fine, or both |
| 324(4) | 427 | Loss/damage of ₹20,000 or more but less than ₹1 lakh | Up to 2 years, or fine, or both |
| 324(5) | 427 | Loss/damage of ₹1 lakh or upwards | Up to 5 years, or fine, or both |
| 324(6) | 440 | Mischief after preparation for causing death, hurt, wrongful restraint, or fear thereof | Up to 5 years + fine |

Note the money thresholds changed: IPC s. 427 used ₹50. The BNS uses ₹20,000 and ₹1 lakh — and the base punishment rose from 3 months (IPC s. 426) to 6 months.

**ss. 325–328 — the aggravated forms:**

- **s. 325 (IPC ss. 428–429)** — mischief by killing, poisoning, maiming or rendering useless any animal: up to 5 years, or fine, or both. (The IPC split animals by value — ₹10 / ₹50 — the BNS does not.)
- **s. 326 (IPC ss. 430–438)** — mischief by injury, inundation, fire or explosive substance, etc.: diminishing the supply of water for agriculture/food/drink, or rendering a public road, bridge, navigable river or channel impassable or less safe — up to 5 years; by fire or explosive substance — up to 7 years + fine; by fire or explosive substance with intent to destroy a house or place of worship — **life, or up to 10 years**.
- **s. 327 (IPC s. 437)** — mischief with intent to destroy or make unsafe a rail, aircraft, decked vessel or one of twenty tons burden: **life, or up to 10 years** + fine.
- **s. 328 (IPC s. 439)** — intentionally running a vessel aground or ashore with intent to commit theft, etc.: up to 10 years + fine.

## 10. Criminal trespass — ss. 329–334 (IPC ss. 441–462 consolidated)

**s. 329(1) — criminal trespass (IPC s. 441).** Whoever enters into or upon property in the possession of another with intent to commit an offence, or to intimidate, insult or annoy any person in possession of such property; or, having lawfully entered, unlawfully remains there with such intent, commits criminal trespass. The **intent** is the gist — a mistaken or innocent entry is not trespass.

**s. 329(2) — house-trespass (IPC s. 442).** Criminal trespass where the property is a building, tent or vessel used as a human dwelling, or a place of worship, or a place for the custody of property.

**s. 329(3)–(4) — punishments.** Criminal trespass: up to 3 months, or fine up to **₹5,000**, or both. House-trespass: up to 1 year, or fine up to **₹5,000**, or both. (The IPC fines were ₹500 and ₹1,000 — both raised to ₹5,000.)

**s. 330 — lurking house-trespass and house-breaking.** (1) *Lurking house-trespass* (IPC s. 443): house-trespass committed having taken precautions to conceal it from the person entitled to exclude the trespasser. (2) *House-breaking* (IPC s. 445): house-trespass effected by entry in any of six described ways (e.g. through a passage made by the trespasser, by opening a locked door, by climbing over a wall, by using a key or instrument to unlock, by scaling, or by passing through a window or other opening not intended for entry).

**s. 331 — the graded punishment ladder (IPC ss. 453–458):**

| Sub-section | Conduct | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| 331(1) | Lurking house-trespass / house-breaking | Up to 2 years + fine |
| 331(2) | Same, after sunset and before sunrise | Up to 3 years + fine |
| 331(3) | Same, in order to commit an offence punishable with imprisonment | Up to 3 years + fine; if theft intended, up to 10 years |
| 331(4) | Same as (3), by night | Up to 5 years + fine; if theft intended, up to 14 years |
| 331(5) | Same, having made preparation for hurt / assault / wrongful restraint / fear thereof | Up to 10 years + fine |
| 331(6) | Same as (5), by night | Up to 14 years + fine |
| 331(7) | Whilst committing the same, causing grievous hurt or attempting to cause death or grievous hurt | Life, or up to 10 years + fine |

**s. 332 (IPC ss. 449–451) — house-trespass in order to commit an offence,** graded by the gravity of the intended offence: punishable with death → life, or RI up to 10 years + fine; punishable with life → up to 10 years + fine; punishable with imprisonment → up to 2 years + fine.

**s. 333 (IPC s. 452) — house-trespass after preparation** for hurt, assault or wrongful restraint: up to 10 years + fine.

**s. 334 (IPC s. 461) — dishonestly breaking open a receptacle** containing property: up to 2 years, or fine, or both.

## 11. Quick-revision checklist

- [ ] Theft = dishonest intention + movable property + out of possession + without consent + moving (s. 303). Repeat conviction: RI 1–5 yrs. Community service only for a first offence < ₹5,000 with return/payment.
- [ ] Snatching (s. 304, new): sudden/quick/forcible taking — theft, not robbery. Force against the *person* makes it robbery.
- [ ] Extortion (s. 308): ordinary extortion now **7 years** (was 3). Preparatory (4)/(6) vs completed (5)/(7); 308(4) = IPC 387, 308(5) = IPC 386.
- [ ] Robbery (s. 309): theft/extortion + violence or fear of **instant** death/hurt/restraint. Highway sunset–sunrise: 14 yrs. Hurt: life/10 yrs.
- [ ] Dacoity (s. 310): **5+ persons conjointly**. Preparation 10 yrs, assembling 7 yrs, gang life/10 yrs. Murder in dacoity: death/life/RI ≥ 10 yrs for all five.
- [ ] ss. 311–312: minimum **7 years** (attempt to cause death/grievous hurt; armed attempt).
- [ ] Misappropriation (s. 314): possession first, dishonesty later — **minimum 6 months** (new). CBT (s. 316): entrustment first — general offence now **5 years** (was 3); public servant/banker etc. life/10 yrs.
- [ ] Cheating (s. 318): deception + dishonest inducement; dishonest intent **at inception** (*Hridaya Ranjan*). (2) 3 yrs; (4) inducing delivery 7 yrs; personation (s. 319) 5 yrs.
