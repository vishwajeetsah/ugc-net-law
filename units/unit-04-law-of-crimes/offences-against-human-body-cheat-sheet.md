# Cheat Sheet — Offences against the Human Body (BNS ss. 100–106)

## Culpable homicide (s. 100) vs murder (s. 101)

| | Culpable homicide | Murder |
|---|---|---|
| Mental element | Intention to cause death / intention to cause injury likely to cause death / knowledge that death is likely | One of the four s. 101 clauses (graver) |
| Rule | Every murder is culpable homicide | Culpable homicide is murder only if a s. 101 clause applies |

## The four clauses of murder (s. 101)

1. **Intention to cause death**
2. **Intention to cause injury known to be likely to cause death** (subjective knowledge)
3. **Intention to cause injury sufficient in the ordinary course of nature to cause death** — *Virsa Singh* (1958): sufficiency is **objective**
4. **Knowledge of imminent danger** — act so dangerous it must in all probability cause death, done **without any excuse**

## The five exceptions (s. 101) — murder → s. 105

| # | Exception | Key test / case |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Grave and sudden provocation | No cooling period; reasonable-person test — *K.M. Nanavati* (1962) |
| 2 | Private defence exceeded | Excess without premeditation, no more harm than necessary |
| 3 | Public servant exceeding powers | Good faith, no ill-will, advancement of public justice |
| 4 | Sudden fight | No premeditation, heat of passion, no undue advantage or cruel manner |
| 5 | Death with consent | Victim above 18, genuine consent |

**Trap:** the exceptions reduce murder to culpable homicide — they never mean "no offence".

## Punishments

| Section | Punishment |
|---|---|
| s. 103(1) (murder) | Death or life + fine |
| s. 103(2) (mob lynching) | Death or life + fine |
| s. 104 (murder by life-convict) | Death or life (remainder of natural life) |
| s. 105, with intent | Life or up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 105, without intent | Up to 10 years + fine |
| s. 106(1) negligence | Up to **5 years** + fine (IPC: 2 years) |
| s. 106(2) hit-and-run | Up to 10 years + fine — enforcement **deferred** (Jan 2024) |

## New / changed — remember these

- **s. 103(2) mob lynching — NEW:** group of **5+** acting in concert, on ground of race, caste/community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief. Background: *Tehseen Poonawalla* (2018).
- **s. 104 — murder by a life-convict (IPC s. 303):** death or life (remainder of natural life); the mandatory death sentence was struck down in *Mithu v. State of Punjab* (1983).
- **s. 106(1) — 5 years** (raised from 2 under IPC s. 304A).
- **s. 106(2) — deferred**, not in force.
- **s. 102 transferred malice** — intention follows the bullet (*R v. Latimer*, 1886).

## Case-law mini-table

| Case | Proposition |
|---|---|
| *Virsa Singh* (1958) | Clause thirdly: sufficiency of injury is objective |
| *Reg v. Govinda* (1876) | Single blow without requisite mental element = culpable homicide, not murder |
| *K.M. Nanavati* (1962) | Grave and sudden provocation: cooling period defeats the exception |
| *Jacob Mathew* (2005) | Criminal negligence = **gross** negligence; expert opinion needed for doctors |
| *Tehseen Poonawalla* (2018) | SC guidelines on mob lynching; background to s. 103(2) |
| *R v. Latimer* (1886) | Transferred malice |
| *R v. Blaue* (1975) | Victim's refusal of treatment does not break causation |

## Exam traps

- **s. 100(b) vs s. 101 clause 2:** s. 100(b) is intention to cause injury *likely* to cause death; s. 101 clause 2 requires the offender to *know* it is likely to cause death.
- **Exception 1 needs suddenness:** time to cool = no exception (*Nanavati*).
- **s. 103(2) needs 5+ persons AND a listed identity ground** — a mob killing for another motive is s. 103(1).
- **s. 106 is not murder:** no intention or knowledge — only rashness/negligence, and it must be gross.
- **Burden:** the accused proves an exception on the **balance of probabilities** (BSA s. 105); the prosecution still proves the killing beyond reasonable doubt.
