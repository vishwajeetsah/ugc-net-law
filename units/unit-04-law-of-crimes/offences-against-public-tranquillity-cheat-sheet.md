# Cheat Sheet — Offences against Public Tranquillity (BNS ss. 189–197)

One-page revision aid for Chapter XI, Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (IPC Chapter VIII, ss. 141–160). (UGC NET Law, Code 58, Unit IV.)

## s. 189 — the five common objects (memorise a–e)

| Object | Wording (short) |
|---|---|
| (a) | Overawe by criminal force: Central/State Government, Parliament/State Legislature, or a public servant in lawful exercise of power |
| (b) | Resist the execution of any law or legal process |
| (c) | Commit mischief, criminal trespass or any other offence |
| (d) | By criminal force: take/obtain possession of property, deprive of right of way/water/incorporeal right, enforce a right or supposed right |
| (e) | By criminal force: compel a person to do what he is not legally bound to do, or omit what he is legally entitled to do |

**Key numbers:** 5+ persons; member 6 months (189(2)); after dispersal order 2 years (189(3)); armed member 2 years (189(4)); assembly *likely to disturb peace* after dispersal order 6 months (189(5)); hirer = as a member (189(6)); harbouring 6 months (189(7)); being hired 6 months (189(8)); hired and armed 2 years (189(9)). Lawful assembly **may become** unlawful (Explanation).

## s. 190 vs s. 3(5) — the two constructive-liability provisions

| | s. 190 (common object) | s. 3(5) (common intention) |
|---|---|---|
| Persons | 5+ (unlawful assembly) | 2+ |
| Basis | Membership + offence in prosecution of common object (or known likely) | Prior concert + participation in the act |
| Overt act by accused | Not needed beyond membership | Needed (participation) |
| Cases | *Mizaji* (1959), *Masalti* (1965), *Lalji* (1989) — object can form on the spur of the moment | *Mahbub Shah* (1945), *Barendra Kumar Ghosh* (1925) |

## Riot vs affray vs unlawful assembly

| | Unlawful assembly (s. 189) | Rioting (s. 191) | Affray (s. 194) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persons | 5+ | 5+ (the assembly) | **2+** |
| Extra element | Common object | **Force or violence** in prosecution of the common object | **Fighting in a public place** |
| Common object needed? | Yes | Yes | No |
| Punishment | 6 months (member) | 2 years; 5 years armed | 1 month / ₹1,000 |

## BNS changes to remember (new-exam gold)

| Change | Old IPC | New BNS |
|---|---|---|
| Armed rioting | s. 148 — 3 years | s. 191(3) — **5 years** |
| Obstructing public servant | s. 152 — 3 years, no minimum fine | s. 195(1) — 3 years + fine **not less than ₹25,000** |
| False information | No equivalent | s. 197(1)(d) — false/misleading info jeopardising sovereignty, unity and integrity or security |
| Electronic communication | Not mentioned | Expressly in ss. 196(1)(a), 197(1) |
| IPC s. 153AA | Carrying arms in procession / mass drill | **Deleted** (never implemented) |

## ss. 196–197 — the two speech offences

| | s. 196 (153A) — promoting enmity | s. 197 (153B) — national integration |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Enmity/hatred between **groups** | A class's **loyalty to India** / citizenship rights |
| Limbs | (a) promote enmity (b) act prejudicial to harmony (c) organised drill/movement to use force | (a) imputation of disloyalty (b) denial of citizenship rights (c) appeal causing disharmony **(d) false/misleading information** — NEW |
| Punishment | 3 years; 5 years in place of worship | 3 years; 5 years in place of worship |
| Key cases | *Bilal Ahmed Kaloo* (1997) — intention essential; *Manzar Sayeed Khan* (2007) — reasonable-person test; *Amish Devgan* (2021) — hate speech | — |

## "Public order" — the three concentric circles (*Lohia*, 1966)

```
Law and order  (largest — every breach of the law)
  └─ Public order  (public peace, safety and tranquillity — the even tempo of community life)
       └─ Security of the State  (smallest — rebellion, waging war, insurrection)
```

- *Lohia* (1960): "public order" = public peace, safety and tranquillity.
- *Romesh Thappar* (1950): security of the State is the only ground that can support the gravest restrictions.
- *S. Rangarajan* (1989): "spark in a powder keg" — the danger must be proximate, not remote.

## BNSS s. 163 (CrPC s. 144) — the preventive companion

District Magistrate's order in urgent cases of nuisance or apprehended danger — **max 2 months**, extendable to **6 months** by the State Government. Preventive, not punitive.

| Case | Holding |
|---|---|
| *Babulal Parate* (1961) | s. 144 constitutional — preventive, temporary |
| *Madhu Limaye* (1971) | Order must be specific, to particular persons/class, based on material |
| *Gulam Abbas* (1982) | No blanket ban; founded on real apprehension |
| *Ramlila Maidan* (2012) | Order + midnight action unjustified; right to sleep under Art. 21 |
| *Thogadia* (2004) | Externment upheld on material showing likely communal tension |

## Exam traps

1. **Four persons can never be an unlawful assembly** — but can still commit the substantive offence.
2. **s. 189(5) is not an unlawful assembly** — it is an assembly *likely to disturb the peace* after a dispersal order.
3. **Armed rioting is 5 years** in the BNS, not the IPC's 3.
4. **s. 193 punishes an omission, with fine only** — never imprisonment.
5. **s. 195(1) has a ₹25,000 minimum fine** — the only minimum fine in the chapter.
6. **s. 196 needs intention to promote enmity** — offensive words alone are not enough (*Bilal Ahmed Kaloo*).
7. **s. 197(1)(d) is the only new clause** — false/misleading information jeopardising sovereignty/unity/integrity/security.
8. **A single murder affects law and order, not public order** (*Lohia*) — public order needs an impact on the community at large.
