## Confused pairs (one line each)

- **Theft vs misappropriation:** dishonesty at the *taking* vs dishonesty *after* lawful possession.
- **Misappropriation vs CBT:** no entrustment (s. 314) vs entrustment/dominion (s. 316).
- **CBT vs cheating:** entrustment + conversion vs deception + inducement to deliver.
- **Cheating vs breach of contract:** dishonest intent **at inception** (*Hridaya Ranjan*, 2000) vs later failure to perform.
- **Extortion vs robbery:** fear of *future* harm vs fear of **instant** harm, in presence, delivery then and there.
- **Robbery vs dacoity:** fewer than 5 vs **5+ persons conjointly** (aiders present count).
- **Snatching vs robbery:** force on the *property* vs force/fear on the *person*.
- **308(4) vs 308(5):** threat in order to extort (7 yrs) vs completed extortion by that threat (10 yrs).
- **310(4) vs 310(5):** preparation (RI 10 yrs) vs assembling (RI 7 yrs).
- **329(3) vs 329(4):** criminal trespass (3 mo) vs house-trespass (1 yr).
- **House-trespass vs lurking house-trespass:** entry into a dwelling vs entry *with precautions to conceal* it.

## Case-law mini-table

| Case | Year | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| *K.N. Mehra v. State of Rajasthan* | 1957 | Dishonest intention is the gist of theft; temporary taking can be theft |
| *Pyare Lal Bhargava v. State of Rajasthan* | 1963 | Taking a file overnight to copy it = theft; return is irrelevant |
| *State of Kerala v. Mathai Verghese* | 1986 | Electricity is "movable property" — can be stolen |
| *Shyam Behari v. State of U.P.* | 1957 | Dacoity: all five must be conjointly concerned; mere presence is not enough |
| *R.K. Dalmia v. Delhi Administration* | 1962 | CBT: entrustment need not be express; "dominion over property" suffices |
| *Hridaya Ranjan Prasad Verma v. State of Bihar* | 2000 | Cheating needs dishonest intent **at inception**; breach of contract is not cheating |
| *State of Kerala v. A. Pareed Pillai* | 1972 | Deception alone is not cheating — dishonest inducement is also required |
| *Sushil Suri v. CBI* | 2011 | No entrustment → cheating, not CBT |
| *Onkar Nath Mishra v. State (NCT of Delhi)* | 2008 | CBT needs proof of entrustment **and** dishonest misappropriation |

## Exam traps

- **s. 308(2) is 7 years, not 3** — the most likely "what changed" question in this chapter.
- **s. 314 has a 6-month minimum** — new under the BNS; IPC s. 403 had none.
- **s. 316(2) is 5 years, not 3** — IPC s. 406 was 3 years.
- **s. 310(4) preparation = 10 years, s. 310(5) assembling = 7 years** — do not swap them.
- **ss. 311 and 312 are minimum sentences** ("not less than 7 years"), not maximums.
- **s. 320 is IPC s. 421, not s. 420** — IPC s. 420 (inducing delivery) is BNS s. **318(4)**.
- **s. 324 amounts:** ₹20,000 and ₹1 lakh, not ₹50 — the IPC threshold is gone.
- **s. 329 fines are ₹5,000** — IPC ss. 447/448 were ₹500/₹1,000.
- **"Instant"** is the word that turns extortion into robbery (s. 309(2)–(3)).
- **Five persons** is the dacoity line — four robbers plus one lookout who aids = dacoity.
