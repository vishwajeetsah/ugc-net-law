# Unit IV — Law of Crimes: Kidnapping, Abduction and Trafficking (BNS ss. 137–146)

Revision note. Chapter VI of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS) — "Of offences affecting the human body" — closes with ss. 137–146, "Of kidnapping, abduction, slavery and forced labour". The BNS compresses IPC ss. 359–374 (fifteen sections) into ten, and moves the woman-and-child offences out to Chapter V (ss. 87, 96–99). The substance is largely retained, but the consolidation itself is the exam point: several IPC sections are now sub-sections of one BNS section, and the aggravated forms (murder, ransom, secret confinement, grievous hurt/slavery) all live in s. 140.

> **Map first:** s. 137 defines kidnapping (from India; from lawful guardianship) and punishes it; s. 138 defines abduction; s. 139 is kidnapping or maiming a child for begging; s. 140 is the aggravated cluster — kidnapping/abducting in order to murder (140(1)), for ransom (140(2)), for secret and wrongful confinement (140(3)), or to subject to grievous hurt, slavery or unnatural lust (140(4)); s. 141 is importation of a girl or boy from a foreign country; s. 142 is wrongfully concealing or keeping in confinement a kidnapped or abducted person; s. 143 is trafficking of a person; s. 144 is exploitation of a trafficked person; s. 145 is habitual dealing in slaves; s. 146 is unlawful compulsory labour.

**PYQ hook:** the kidnapping–abduction distinction and the "taking" cases (*S. Varadarajan*, *Thakorlal Vadgama*, *Raja Ram*) are the most-asked points in this cluster; s. 140(2) (ransom — death or life) and the s. 143 trafficking punishment ladder (2013 amendment) are the regular follow-ups. Art. 23 of the Constitution (traffic in human beings and forced labour) is the constitutional anchor that PYQs pair with ss. 143–146.

## 1. Kidnapping — s. 137 (IPC ss. 359–361 + 363)

Kidnapping is of **two kinds only**:

| Kind | BNS | IPC | Essentials |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kidnapping from India** | s. 137(1)(a) | s. 360 | Conveying a person beyond the limits of India **without the consent** of that person, or of some person legally authorised to consent on his behalf |
| **Kidnapping from lawful guardianship** | s. 137(1)(b) | s. 361 | **Taking or enticing** a child (male under 16, female under 18) or a person of unsound mind, **out of the keeping of the lawful guardian**, without the guardian's consent |

**Punishment — s. 137(2):** imprisonment of either description up to **7 years** and fine.

Five points on kidnapping from lawful guardianship — the exam favourite:

1. **"Taking" needs an active role.** The accused must take or entice; merely allowing a minor who leaves on her own to accompany him is not "taking" — *S. Varadarajan v. State of Madras* (1965). In that case a minor girl left her father's house of her own accord and joined the accused; the Supreme Court acquitted, because there was no taking.
2. **But persuasion is taking.** Inducing a minor to leave by persuasion, allurement or blandishment *is* taking — *Thakorlal D. Vadgama v. State of Gujarat* (1973). The line is between the accused's active inducement and the minor's independent decision.
3. **Consent of the minor is irrelevant** — the consent that matters is the guardian's. A minor cannot consent to being taken out of guardianship.
4. **The guardian's keeping need not be physical custody** — a minor at school, or in the care of another with the guardian's authority, is still in the guardian's keeping.
5. **Motive is irrelevant** for the basic offence. Even a benevolent motive (e.g. taking a child to protect it) does not negate kidnapping; motive matters only at sentencing, or under the aggravated sections.

**Kidnapping vs abduction — the core distinction:**

| | Kidnapping (s. 137) | Abduction (s. 138) |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Only a **child** (male < 16, female < 18) or a person of unsound mind (for guardianship); any person (for from India) | **Any person**, of any age |
| **Act** | Taking or enticing out of keeping | Compelling by **force**, or inducing by **deceitful means**, to go from any place |
| **Consent** | Guardian's consent is the test | Victim's consent obtained by deceit is no consent |
| **Punishment** | Punishable **in itself** (s. 137(2)) | **Not punishable in itself** — punished only when done for a purpose the law names (ss. 87, 97, 140) |

## 2. Abduction — s. 138 (IPC s. 362)

> "Whoever by force compels, or by any deceitful means induces, any person to go from any place, is said to abduct that person."

Two elements: (a) **force** — actual physical force or threat of force; or (b) **deceitful means** — inducing by fraud or trick. The victim must actually **go** from the place — movement is essential. Unlike kidnapping, abduction can be of an adult, and it is **not an offence by itself**: it becomes punishable only when coupled with a purpose — to murder (s. 140(1)), for ransom (s. 140(2)), for secret confinement (s. 140(3)), to subject to grievous hurt/slavery (s. 140(4)), to compel marriage (s. 87), or to steal from a child under ten (s. 97).

**Exam trap:** do not call every taking of an adult "kidnapping". An adult cannot be kidnapped from lawful guardianship; the taking of an adult by force or deceit is abduction, punishable only through its purpose-section.

## 3. Kidnapping or maiming a child for begging — s. 139 (IPC s. 363A)

Whoever kidnaps a child **for the purpose of begging**, or maims a child for that purpose, is punished with rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years** and fine; if the child is **maimed**, the punishment is imprisonment for **life**. The section targets the organised exploitation of children as beggars — the maiming aggravation reflects the practice of deliberately disabling children to increase begging returns.

## 4. The aggravated cluster — s. 140 (IPC ss. 364, 364A, 365, 367)

One section, four purposes. The act is kidnapping or abduction; the **purpose** fixes the punishment:

| Sub-section | Purpose | IPC | Punishment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **s. 140(1)** | In order to **murder**, or so that the person may be murdered, or put in danger of being murdered | s. 364 | Imprisonment for **life**, or rigorous imprisonment up to **10 years**, and fine |
| **s. 140(2)** | For **ransom** — to demand money or valuable security, or to compel any person to do or abstain from doing anything | s. 364A | **Death**, or imprisonment for **life**, and fine |
| **s. 140(3)** | To be **secretly and wrongfully confined** | s. 365 | Imprisonment up to **7 years** and fine |
| **s. 140(4)** | To subject the person to **grievous hurt, slavery, or the unnatural lust** of any person | s. 367 | Imprisonment up to **10 years** and fine |

**s. 140(2) is the heaviest provision in this cluster** — death or life — and the one PYQs test most. Note the ransom definition is wide: it covers not only a demand for money but compelling any person to do or abstain from doing anything. The 1993 insertion of IPC s. 364A (the anti-kidnapping-for-ransom amendment) survives unchanged in substance.

## 5. Importation and concealment — ss. 141–142

- **s. 141 (IPC s. 366B):** importation of a **girl under 21 years** or a **boy under 18 years** from a foreign country, knowing it to be likely that the person will be forced or seduced to illicit intercourse with another person — imprisonment up to **10 years** and fine. (The age thresholds differ by sex — a favourite trap.)
- **s. 142 (IPC s. 368):** wrongfully **concealing or keeping in confinement** a kidnapped or abducted person — punished with the **same punishment as the kidnapping or abduction itself**. The concealer need not have done the taking; knowingly keeping the victim is enough.

## 6. Trafficking — ss. 143–144 (IPC ss. 370–370A)

Inserted by the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2013, following the Justice Verma Committee, ss. 143–144 give effect to Art. 23 of the Constitution (prohibition of traffic in human beings and forced labour) and to India's obligations under the UN Trafficking Protocol.

**s. 143 — trafficking of a person.** "Trafficking" means recruiting, transporting, harbouring, transferring or receiving a person, by (a) threats, (b) force or coercion, (c) abduction, (d) fraud or deception, (e) abuse of power, or (f) inducement (including giving or receiving payments or benefits to achieve the consent of a person having control over another person) — for the purpose of **exploitation**. "Exploitation" expressly includes prostitution or other sexual exploitation, forced labour, slavery or servitude, and removal of organs. **The victim's consent is immaterial** once any of the listed means is used.

| Sub-section | Case | Punishment |
|---|---|---|
| s. 143(1) | Trafficking of a person | Rigorous imprisonment **7–10 years** + fine |
| s. 143(2) | Trafficking of **more than one** person | Rigorous imprisonment **10 years to life** + fine |
| s. 143(3) | Trafficking of a **minor** | Rigorous imprisonment **10 years to life** + fine |
| s. 143(4) | Trafficking of **more than one minor** | Rigorous imprisonment **14 years to life** + fine |
| s. 143(5) | **Repeat offender** (convicted more than once) | Imprisonment for **life** (remainder of natural life) + fine |

**s. 144 — exploitation of a trafficked person (IPC s. 370A).** Whoever, **knowingly or having reason to believe** that a person has been trafficked, engages that person for sexual exploitation: rigorous imprisonment **5–7 years** + fine; if the trafficked person is a **minor**, rigorous imprisonment **5–7 years** + fine. (Both limbs carry the same 5–7 year range — the minority is an aggravating fact, not a separate range.)

**Constitutional and statutory context:**
- **Art. 23** prohibits traffic in human beings, begar and similar forms of forced labour; **Art. 24** prohibits employment of children below 14 in factories, mines or hazardous work.
- **Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976** abolished bonded labour; **Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956** targets commercial sexual exploitation.
- *Vishal Jeet v. Union of India* (1990): the Supreme Court issued directions for the rescue and rehabilitation of children forced into prostitution, treating trafficking as a violation of Arts. 21 and 23.
- *Pinki v. State of Uttar Pradesh* (2025): one of the Supreme Court's first sustained engagements with the kidnapping and trafficking framework after the BNS came into force — the old IPC-era principles on "taking" and trafficking continue to govern under the new numbering.

## 7. Slavery and forced labour — ss. 145–146 (IPC ss. 371, 374)

- **s. 145 (IPC s. 371):** habitual dealing in **slaves** — imprisonment for **life**, or up to **10 years**, and fine. The section punishes the *trade* (importing, exporting, removing, buying, selling or disposing of persons as slaves), habitually.
- **s. 146 (IPC s. 374):** **unlawful compulsory labour** — whoever unlawfully compels any person to labour against the will of that person: imprisonment up to **1 year**, or fine, or both.

**The forced-labour cases:**
- *People's Union for Democratic Rights (PUDR) v. Union of India* (1982): "forced labour" under Art. 23 is not limited to physical compulsion — it includes labour extracted for **less than the minimum wage** (begar). Any labour exacted by economic compulsion, where the worker has no real choice, is forced labour.
- *Bandhua Mukti Morcha v. Union of India* (1984): the Court treated bonded labour as a violation of Art. 21 and Art. 23 together, and laid down that the State must identify, release and rehabilitate bonded labourers; the burden of proof on the question of bondedness was eased for the worker.

## 8. The related Chapter V offences — cross-reference

The woman-and-child kidnapping offences moved to Chapter V ("Of offences against woman and child") and are examined with this cluster:

| BNS | Offence | IPC |
|---|---|---|
| s. 87 | Kidnapping, abducting or inducing a woman to compel her marriage, etc. | s. 366 |
| s. 96 | Procuration of a child (inducing a child to go from any place to do an act with intent that the child may be forced/seduced to illicit intercourse) | s. 366A |
| s. 97 | Kidnapping or abducting a child **under ten years** with intent to steal from its person | s. 369 |
| s. 98 | Selling a child for purposes of prostitution, etc. | s. 372 |
| s. 99 | Buying a child for purposes of prostitution, etc. (minimum punishment raised to **7 years** under the BNS) | s. 373 |

## 9. Confused pairs — the exam traps

1. **Kidnapping vs abduction:** age/keeping vs force-or-deceit; punishable in itself vs punishable only through a purpose-section.
2. **s. 137(2) vs s. 140(2):** simple kidnapping is up to 7 years; kidnapping **for ransom** is death or life. The purpose changes everything.
3. **"Taking" (Varadarajan) vs "enticing" (Vadgama):** a minor who leaves on her own and is merely accompanied is not "taken"; a minor persuaded or induced to leave is taken.
4. **s. 143 vs s. 144:** s. 143 punishes the **trafficker** (7 years up to life, graded by number and age); s. 144 punishes the **exploiter** who knowingly uses a trafficked person (5–7 years).
5. **s. 141 vs s. 97:** s. 141 is importation of a girl under 21 / boy under 18 **from a foreign country**; s. 97 is kidnapping a child **under ten** to steal from its person.
6. **s. 145 vs s. 146:** habitual dealing in slaves (life/10 years) vs a single act of unlawful compulsory labour (up to 1 year).
7. **"Kidnapping from India" vs "from lawful guardianship":** the first needs no minority — any person conveyed out of India without consent; the second needs a child or person of unsound mind taken from the guardian's keeping.

## Quick revision checklist

- [ ] Two kinds of kidnapping — s. 137(1)(a) from India, s. 137(1)(b) from lawful guardianship; punishment s. 137(2): up to 7 years + fine.
- [ ] Abduction (s. 138): force or deceitful means, to go from any place; not punishable alone.
- [ ] "Taking" trio: *Varadarajan* (1965) — no taking where the minor leaves on her own; *Vadgama* (1973) — persuasion is taking; *Raja Ram* (1973) — merely allowing accompaniment is not taking.
- [ ] s. 139: child for begging — 10 years; maimed — life.
- [ ] s. 140 ladder: (1) murder — life/10 years; (2) ransom — death or life; (3) secret confinement — 7 years; (4) grievous hurt/slavery — 10 years.
- [ ] s. 141: importation — girl < 21, boy < 18, up to 10 years.
- [ ] s. 142: concealing a kidnapped person — same punishment as the kidnapping.
- [ ] s. 143 ladder: 7–10 years; more than one person 10 years–life; minor 10 years–life; more than one minor 14 years–life; repeat offender life.
- [ ] s. 144: exploitation of trafficked person — 5–7 years (adult or minor).
- [ ] s. 145: habitual slave dealing — life/10 years; s. 146: unlawful compulsory labour — up to 1 year.
- [ ] Art. 23 (traffic in human beings, begar) + Art. 24; *PUDR* (1982) and *Bandhua Mukti Morcha* (1984) on forced/bonded labour; *Vishal Jeet* (1990) on trafficking.
