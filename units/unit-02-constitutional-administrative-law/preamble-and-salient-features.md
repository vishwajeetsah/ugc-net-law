# Unit II — Constitutional & Administrative Law: Preamble and Salient Features of the Indian Constitution

Revision note. The Preamble and the basic-structure chronology are among the most repeated Unit II themes: Kesavananda Bharati on the Preamble opened the August 2024 paper as Q.1, and the amendment-case chronology was asked in December 2023 and again in December 2025.

## 1. The Preamble

The Preamble is the introduction to the Constitution, adopted on **26 November 1949** and coming into force on **26 January 1950**. It begins "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA" — the source of the Constitution's authority is the people, not any external sovereign.

**The text (as amended):** "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens: **JUSTICE**, social, economic and political; **LIBERTY** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation..."

**The 42nd Amendment, 1976** inserted three words: "SOCIALIST", "SECULAR", and "integrity" (so "unity of the Nation" became "unity and integrity of the Nation").

**The Preamble's roots:** it is based on the **Objectives Resolution** moved by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13 December 1946 and adopted by the Constituent Assembly on 22 January 1947.

### Is the Preamble part of the Constitution?

| Case | Position |
|---|---|
| *In re Berubari Union* (1960) | Preamble is **not** a part of the Constitution; it cannot be a source of substantive power |
| *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973) | Overruled *Berubari* on this point — the Preamble **is** a part of the Constitution |
| *S.R. Bommai v. Union of India* (1994) | Secularism is part of the basic structure of the Constitution |

**What the Preamble does:** it is a key to the minds of the framers and an interpretive aid — where the text is ambiguous, it is read in the light of the Preamble's ideals. It is **not** itself enforceable in a court, and it cannot override an express constitutional provision.

## 2. The basic-structure chronology (must memorise in order)

| Year | Case / event | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| 1951 | *Shankari Prasad v. Union of India* | Parliament **can** amend Fundamental Rights; Art. 13 does not apply to amendments under Art. 368 |
| 1965 | *Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan* | Reaffirmed *Shankari Prasad* |
| 1967 | *Golaknath v. State of Punjab* (11-judge bench) | Parliament **cannot** amend Fundamental Rights; reversed *Shankari Prasad* |
| 1971 | 24th Amendment | Expressly restored Parliament's power to amend any provision, including Fundamental Rights; inserted Art. 13(4) |
| 1973 | *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (13-judge bench, 7:6) | Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, **but not its basic structure**; upheld the 24th Amendment; Preamble is part of the Constitution |
| 1980 | *Minerva Mills v. Union of India* | Struck down clauses (4) and (5) of Art. 368 inserted by the 42nd Amendment; judicial review is part of the basic structure; harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles |

**PYQ hook:** the chronology Shankari Prasad → Sajjan Singh → Golaknath → Kesavananda was asked in December 2023 and again in December 2025. Articles 368 (amendment procedure) and 13 (laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights) were asked together in October 2022.

## 3. Salient features of the Constitution

| Feature | Note |
|---|---|
| Lengthiest written constitution | Originally 395 Articles in 22 Parts and 8 Schedules; amendments have added Articles, Parts and Schedules since |
| Partly rigid, partly flexible | Some provisions amendable by simple majority, most by special majority, a few by special majority plus ratification by half the States (Art. 368) |
| Federal system with a unitary bias | Division of powers (Union, State and Concurrent Lists, Seventh Schedule), but a strong Centre — single citizenship, integrated judiciary, all-India services, emergency provisions |
| Parliamentary government | Real executive power with the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, responsible to the Lok Sabha (Arts. 74–75) |
| Fundamental Rights (Part III, Arts. 12–35) | Justiciable; enforceable by the Supreme Court (Art. 32) and High Courts (Art. 226) |
| Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Arts. 36–51) | Non-justiciable guidelines for governance; the Constitution calls them "fundamental in the governance of the country" (Art. 37) |
| Fundamental Duties (Part IVA, Art. 51A) | Added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976; originally 10 duties, an 11th added by the 86th Amendment, 2002 |
| Secular state | No state religion; equal treatment of all religions — the word "secular" was added to the Preamble in 1976, but the secular character predates it |
| Independent and integrated judiciary | One system of courts from the Supreme Court down; judges' tenure and salaries protected |
| Universal adult franchise | Every citizen aged 18 or above may vote (Art. 326; voting age lowered from 21 to 18 by the 61st Amendment, 1989) |
| Single citizenship | One citizenship for the whole country, unlike the dual citizenship of the USA |
| Emergency provisions (Part XVIII, Arts. 352–360) | National, State (President's Rule) and financial emergencies |
| Three-tier government | 73rd and 74th Amendments, 1992 — Panchayats and Municipalities |
| Independent constitutional bodies | Election Commission, Comptroller and Auditor-General, Union Public Service Commission, Finance Commission |

**PYQ hook:** *S.R. Bommai* (1994) — secularism as a basic feature and President's Rule subject to judicial review — was asked in December 2025.

## 4. Quick revision checklist

- Preamble adopted 26 Nov 1949; in force 26 Jan 1950; based on the Objectives Resolution (Nehru, 13 Dec 1946).
- 42nd Amendment (1976): added "Socialist", "Secular", "integrity".
- *Berubari* (1960): Preamble not part of the Constitution → overruled by *Kesavananda* (1973).
- Basic-structure chronology: Shankari Prasad (1951) → Sajjan Singh (1965) → Golaknath (1967) → 24th Amendment (1971) → Kesavananda Bharati (1973) → Minerva Mills (1980).
- Basic structure limits Parliament's amending power under Art. 368; it does not make any provision unamendable outright.
- Do not confuse: the Preamble is **not** enforceable, and it cannot override an express provision — but it guides interpretation.
